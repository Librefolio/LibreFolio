## Chronology

### 2026-09-07 — Original backlog and first expansion

**Original backlog — historical scope.** The earliest committed snapshot described one M–L, self-contained feature centered on the Directa study: accept PAC allocation inputs, support whole-unit purchases and either number-of-units or maximum-euro entry, expose a backend API and a frontend, and leave MCP export to a later phase. It was explicitly presented as independent of other work and as the large feature of a round.

**Same-day expansion — confirmed direction at the time.** The current backlog/status page records that planning broadened the request beyond a euro, buy-only PAC calculator into three coordinated tracks:

1. a generic backend Tool plugin platform;
2. one shared mathematical model capable of PAC, rebalancing, optional sales, native-currency cash, explicit FX, costs, buffers, limits, and exact Decimal handling;
3. a custom Tool hub/editor/report UI with authorized domain snapshots rather than Tool-owned portfolio lookups.

The expansion also established that PAC Planning in AI Export, the risk optimizer, and Scheduled Investment were different systems, not substitutes for this allocator. MCP remained future work and was not part of the round.

### 2026-09-08 — Platform boundary and deliberately narrow P1

**Confirmed decisions at that checkpoint.** The Tool platform was to be custom-first: Pydantic/TypeAdapter models were the schema source, catalog and compute were shared platform services, each item ran in an isolated worker, diagnostics were sanitized for authenticated users, and the compute plugin remained pure. Portfolio, Asset, Broker, and FX data had to be collected through their domain APIs before compute; no `/tools/prefill` shadow API and no DB/provider lookup from the mathematical worker were allowed.

The developer authorized only the narrow initial P1 core: strict `operation="analyze"` codecs, deterministic Decimal normalization, initial-state valuation and per-row gaps, typed partial facts, and backend tests. Solver, orders, sales, fees, execution FX, portfolio copies, Broker migration, UI, generated client, and docs were explicitly outside that authorization.

The same dated planning record separated quantity granularity from monetary granularity. A positive exact monetary step was an operational constraint, not display precision, and was not restricted to powers of ten. This became important later because the final design moved monetary order granularity from a contribution row to the operational Broker/currency profile.

### 2026-09-09 to 2026-09-10 — P1 core and Tool-platform integration

**Historical prototype.** The P1 core was implemented as a pure Decimal pipeline: schema → normalize → evaluate → report. It used opaque row identity, separate instrument identity, native cash and contribution vectors, explicit valuation FX, exact target totals, and honest states `ready`, `needs_input`, `invalid`, and `unsupported`. It deliberately emitted `trade_feasibility="not_evaluated"` and `optimization="not_run"`, because it did not solve trades.

## Decision record D1–D12 and latest corrections

### D1 — Two distinct tools, one shared planning core

**Confirmed decision.** PAC and Rebalancer remain separate public tools and UI flows. PAC answers how selected liquidity should become executable purchases; Rebalancer answers which purchases, and optionally sales, move the entire invested portfolio toward final target weights. They share normalization, Broker/FX modeling, quantization, evaluator, and solver, and retain the two existing `tool_code` values.

**Correction.** Round 4 correctly separated the services, but both still performed analysis-only `analyze`. The final contract changes each service to one `operation="plan"` that returns operational funding, FX, and Broker order actions.

### D2 — Funding sources are explicit and may be funding-only

**Confirmed decision.** Step 2 models three mutually exclusive source kinds: new external liquidity, an existing account, or a scenario-only manual account. A source may fund the plan without being an execution Broker. Transfer routes must be declared in the snapshot; funding-only sources are excluded from Asset routing.

**Correction.** The prior cash/contribution vectors did not represent source identity or transfers. The final model produces explicit source-to-Broker funding actions and prevents a bank or other source from becoming an execution venue merely because it holds cash.

### D3 — Only selected partial cash is available

**Confirmed decision.** For every source and currency, the user selects an amount bounded by the known available balance. The solver may use only that amount, never the full account balance by implication. If an entity is both source and operating Broker, its identity is reused and the cash is counted once; false self-transfer is forbidden.

**Correction.** Round 2 and Round 4 selected Broker cash at the balance/vector level. The final design allows partial amounts, makes unselected cash invisible to the solver, and separates newly added liquidity from existing cash so neither is duplicated.

### D4 — Broker routing is explicit Asset×Broker configuration

**Confirmed decision.** Operating Brokers are chosen independently of funding sources. Each Asset has an allow-list of Brokers and numeric priorities; equal priorities express no preference. Routing outside the list is forbidden. Asset→Broker choice is configuration, not a strategy. At otherwise equal score and priority, estimated fees break ties, followed only by a stable Broker identifier for determinism.

**Correction.** Early plans explicitly had no Broker routing, and Round 4 retained Broker contexts mainly as custody facts. The operational design turns selected Asset×Broker compatibility into a hard input while keeping policy choices separate.

### D5 — “Broker capabilities use one order-entry mode” (superseded)

**Superseded historical design.** The earlier artifact made each operating Broker either `existing_broker` or scenario-only `manual_broker`, then used one user/Broker input named `order_entry_mode` for BUY and SELL. It distinguished `integer_units` from `cash_amount` and had already moved monetary stepping away from contribution rows. That input model is no longer current.

**Current authoritative correction.** There is no parallel `order_entry_mode`,
fractional Boolean, `quantity_step`, or separate fractional-sale capability. Each
operating Broker/currency has one enum `order_instruction_kind`:

- `whole_quantity`: integer share count;
- `monetary_amount`: exact cash notional plus positive `order_amount_step`.

The response may discriminate the derived instruction as `order_instruction.kind = share_quantity | cash_amount`. That discriminant is output-only, not user configuration. Existing Broker/domain data may prefill known facts, but editable execution parameters belong to the per-run snapshot. A manual Broker remains scenario-only.

### D6 — Fees and FX costs participate in native-currency cash feasibility

**Confirmed decision.** BUY and SELL use independent fee profiles. Each may combine a fixed component with a percentage and optional minimum/maximum on the percentage component; zero values are valid, so free PAC BUY does not zero SELL fees. FX spread, optional buffer, conversion fee, order fee, accounting rounding, and the estimated SELL tax reserve remain distinct. A plan is cash-feasible only after all applicable amounts are charged or reserved in the correct native ledger.

**Correction.** Round 4 omitted fees and execution FX entirely. The first operational draft also allocated gross cash and subtracted costs too late. The corrected design solves for investable capital net of costs and carries costs through the ledger rather than labeling them investment.

**Second-review clarification.** For a fractional BUY, `M_buy` is the fee-exclusive cash amount entered at the Broker and quantized by `order_amount_step`; estimated quantity is `M_buy / P^(chg)`, cash debit is the rounded `M_buy` plus the separately posted BUY fee, and invested/chart value uses `P^(mid)`. The charge-versus-mid difference is the FX spread cost, not another debit. For a fractional SELL, `M_sell` is the requested gross cash credit, quantized by the same Broker/currency step; estimated quantity is `M_sell / P^(sell)` and must not exceed exact custody. The canonical input name is `fx_buffer_rate`, the canonical monetary output name is `fx_buffer_amount`, and neither denotes a fee.

### D7 — “PMC, tax regime, carried losses, and withholding are explicit facts” (superseded)

**Superseded historical design.** The earlier artifact required a `withholding_kind`, contemplated a user-provided withholding schedule, and left its minimum format as an open product blocker. That input and blocker are removed. This is also the prior open D20 SELL-tax product gate named by the developer's first authoritative correction.

**Current authoritative correction.** The snapshot includes exact holdings and PMC per Asset×Broker with currency, date, and provenance; Broker tax regime as context; known carried losses with currency/date; and an Asset input `capital_gains_tax_rate`. The UI prefills that rate to **26%**, allows the user to edit it, and sends it on the wire as a ratio.

For every Asset×Broker SELL, the backend:

1. computes gross proceeds and applies the SELL fee;
2. computes only the positive residual gain against PMC;
3. applies that Asset's `capital_gains_tax_rate` to the positive gain;
4. rounds the estimated tax reserve in the ledger currency;
5. defines net sell cash as gross proceeds minus SELL fee minus estimated tax reserve; and
6. reserves that tax amount before any SELL proceeds are reused for BUY.

Tax regime does not alter the reserve amount and v1 performs no hidden tax optimization or compensation. Carried losses remain captured, but v1 must not silently offset them without an approved tax-aware policy. There is no user-provided `withholding_kind`, withholding schedule, or open SELL-tax product gate. The backend derives output `withholding_kind` from `tax_regime`: `broker_withheld` for `administered`, `self_reserved` for `declarative_free`. Both exclude the reserve from BUY funding; only `self_reserved` remains part of expected physical account cash while staying unavailable to the plan.

### D8 — The v1 strategy shortlist is deliberately small

**Confirmed decision.** PAC offers `proportional` and `min_fragmentation`. Rebalancer offers `invest_only` by default and `invest_and_sell`. A separate sell-only mode is unnecessary because `invest_and_sell` with zero new liquidity covers it. Minimum turnover, order count, priority, and fees are deterministic objective levels or tie-breaks, not extra user-facing modes.

**Correction.** Earlier material mixed objectives, presets, and future fiscal strategies. The final shortlist keeps only four user-facing choices and defers tax-aware, closure, consolidation, and transfer-optimization strategies.

**Second-review clarification.** `invest_and_sell` is deliberately sequential, not a global joint BUY/SELL optimum: first solve and freeze the best `invest_only` phase, then admit SELL of overweight holdings only to reduce the remaining gap, with turnover/cost/order-count tie-breaks. The exhaustive oracle must reproduce that same phase ordering rather than compare against a different joint objective.

### D9 — The UI is single-flight

**Confirmed decision.** The wizard gathers all facts and policy choices, then submits one complete request. Domain APIs may prefill facts during editing, but there is no planner preview, intermediate solver, or hidden compute at target/FX steps. If the result is unsatisfactory, the user edits inputs and starts a new calculation.

**Correction.** The analysis-only UI encouraged a staged mental model in which target analysis appeared complete before routing, fee, and funding facts existed. The final flow treats the actual plan as unknowable until the single backend calculation.

### D10 — Alternatives and solver states use normalized enums, not interacting booleans

**Confirmed decision.** Exclusive alternatives are discriminated unions. Solver status is split into orthogonal `outcome`, `proof`, and `stop_reason`; target deviation, cash deficit, and optimality gap remain numbers, not overloaded statuses. “Optimal” is permitted only with proof.

**Correction.** The design rejects parallel flags such as
`is_new`/`is_existing`/`is_manual`, fractional BUY/SELL flags, and combined
timeout/quality labels. Broker/currency input/output share one
`order_instruction_kind` enum.

**Second-review clarification.** No hidden `epsilon` or tolerance may relax the Decimal objective tuple or classify a target as reached. `no_op` is the feasible empty plan when no allowed action improves the objective—including cash below a conditional minimum or cash with no useful route. `infeasible_proven` is reserved for proof that explicit hard constraints, such as an incompatible positive `required_min_notional`, empty the entire domain. Missing facts remain `needs_input`; a limit without an incumbent remains `no_incumbent`.

### D11 — One technical valuation currency is required but is not an order currency

**Confirmed decision.** A common numeraire is required to compare and aggregate instruments across currencies. It is distinct from quote currency, Broker debit currency, and every native cash ledger. All funding, orders, transfers, and FX actions remain in native currencies.

**Current authoritative correction.** P1's `report_currency` is replaced by `valuation_currency`. It is a visible Step 1 user field, prefilled from the logged-in user's default currency, editable before calculation, and explicitly included in the submitted snapshot. The worker performs no user/default/domain lookup. The result states which valuation currency and rate/date produced the comparative weights.

### D12 — Complete atomic snapshot and unreleased clean break

**Confirmed decision.** Every price, balance, holding, PMC, fee, Broker/currency capability and order step, Asset capital-gains tax rate, tax-regime and carried-loss context, FX quote, route, `valuation_currency`, and relevant provenance is materialized in one snapshot. The worker performs no DB query, user-default lookup, domain-service call, or provider fetch during `compute`; only cancellation/limit checkpoints are runtime context. Equal snapshots and limits must produce deterministic numeric results.

Existing Broker/domain data may prefill known facts before submission. Remaining Broker execution parameters are per-run snapshot inputs. V1 adds no Broker operational-profile persistence or migration, and a manual Broker is scenario-only. Likewise, `capital_gains_tax_rate` is an Asset scenario input in v1 rather than a new persisted Asset field.

Because the product is unreleased, the final public contract remains `1.0.0` while replacing the rejected P1 wholesale. The implementation is to remove `operation="analyze"`, replace `report_currency` with the explicit `valuation_currency`, remove the superseded `order_entry_mode` and withholding inputs, and remove old schemas/renderers/adapters and tests/i18n that exist only for the discarded behavior. No compatibility union, feature flag, fallback, parallel endpoint, or `2.0.0` legacy track is required.

**Correction.** This resolves two earlier ambiguities: domain lookups cannot occur during compute, and preservation of a tested prototype does not create a public compatibility obligation.

**Second-review clarification.** Targets and residuals belong to `AssetPlanSummary`, aggregated across every selected Broker; Broker plans contain execution rows and must not duplicate a route-level target. ## Mathematical audit — eight blocker classes and incorporated design fixes

The 2026-09-15 audit is evidence about the design mathematics, not a solver implementation or proof that a production solver passes these conditions.

| Blocker found | Why it blocked correctness | Fix incorporated into the design |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Budget was not net of costs | Allocating all gross cash before fees, FX cost, buffer, and SELL tax reserves can create orders the account cannot fund. | Define reference investable capital as the maximum amount satisfying investment plus BUY fees, FX cost, and FX buffer within selected cash; when SELL proceeds are present, admit only proceeds net of SELL fee and estimated tax reserve; enforce the complete cash law before feasibility. |
| 2. Multi-currency units were mixed | Summing EUR, USD, and other balances as bare numbers makes constraints dimensionally invalid. | Maintain a separate native ledger for every Broker/currency. Use the technical valuation currency only for objectives, target comparisons, and charts. |
| 3. FX credits were not coupled to debits | A model could create destination cash without consuming source cash. | Every allowed conversion has a source debit and a derived destination credit at an effective rate; allow only declared pairs, one funding hop, and no active cycle/arbitrage. |
| 4. SELL proceeds were absent from cash conservation | Sales could reduce holdings without financing subsequent BUY orders, or could overstate reusable cash by ignoring tax reserve. | For each Asset×Broker SELL, credit only net sell cash—gross proceeds minus SELL fee minus the ledger-rounded estimated tax reserve—to the correct Broker/currency ledger, and reserve tax before any BUY reuses the proceeds. |
| 5. SELL was not bounded by custody | Aggregated sales could exceed inventory or sell on the wrong Broker. | Keep exact `h[a,b]` inventory per Asset×Broker and cap every SELL by that custody. Apply the same Broker/currency `order_instruction_kind` to BUY and SELL and forbid short positions. |
| 6. `K ≥ K*` was treated as generally sufficient | The continuous, free, unquantized buy-only relaxation does not prove feasibility once fees, routing, minimums, quantization, and FX are present. | Retain `F*`, `K*`, and theoretical buys only as diagnostics under explicit preconditions; solve the full discrete cash model and report deviation/deficit rather than claiming exact feasibility. |
| 7. Fragmentation minimization could choose the zero plan | Minimizing split/order counts without preserving investment lets no orders win trivially. | Link binary `y[a,b]` to actual order variables, require investment at least equal to the proportional baseline, constrain target deviation within deterministic tolerance, then optimize split count, order count, priority, fees, and stable tie-break lexicographically. |
| 8. Residual post-optimization contradicted the base solution | A second optimization could silently move, reduce, or replace base orders while being presented as an additive improvement. | Freeze base decisions and permit only nonnegative increments. The post-step pays incremental debit and fee from the corresponding residual ledger, introduces no sales, preserves tolerance and fragmentation rules, and reports investment delta separately from added fees. |

The audit also required outcome/proof/stop-reason orthogonality and explicit tolerances derived from order granularity. Those corrections are part of the artifact. They remain unimplemented in the permitted evidence.

The later authoritative correction refines blocker 4 with the current SELL cash law:

- `positive_gain = max(0, sold_quantity × (sell_price − PMC))`;
- `estimated_tax_reserve = round_in_ledger_currency(positive_gain × capital_gains_tax_rate)`;
- `net_sell_cash = gross_proceeds − sell_fee − estimated_tax_reserve`.

The Asset tax rate is supplied on the wire as a ratio. The reserve is deducted before proceeds can fund BUY. Tax regime and carried losses do not alter these equations in v1, and the worker does not infer an offset.

| # | Finding | Incorporated current-design fix |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Fractional cash orders conflated the cash entered at the Broker with mid-valued investment and could charge spread twice. | Fractional BUY `M_buy` is the fee-exclusive, step-quantized cash amount entered at the Broker. Estimated quantity is `M_buy / P^(chg)`, order debit is rounded `M_buy`, BUY fee is separate, and investment/chart value is that quantity at `P^(mid)`; the charge-minus-mid difference is spread cost, not a second debit. Fractional SELL `M_sell` is a step-quantized requested gross credit; estimated quantity is `M_sell / P^(sell)`, while mid value, spread, fee, and tax reserve remain separate facts. |
| 2 | A single precomputed `B_ref` was circular because fee, FX, buffer, and active order rows depend on the candidate being solved. | Solve native cash ledgers directly. `K_reachable` is only gross cash that can reach at least one eligible BUY within declared routes/caps. If exposed, `B_ref(x) = K_reachable − C_buy(x) − C_FX(x) − H_FX(x)` is an ex-post, candidate-dependent diagnostic—not an input denominator or bound used to construct `x`. |
| 3 | A hidden epsilon could silently relax target quality or let a secondary policy degrade the base objective. | Use exact Decimal comparisons and explicitly freeze the lexicographic base tuple (`max` Asset deviation, then squared deviation sum). | 4 | A feasible empty plan was being confused with hard-constraint infeasibility. | `no_op` means the empty plan is feasible and no allowed action improves the objective, including cash below a conditional minimum or no useful route. `infeasible_proven` requires proof that explicit hard constraints empty the entire domain. | 5 | SELL cash could be double-counted by posting both gross proceeds and derived net proceeds. | The native ledger credits gross SELL proceeds once, then subtracts SELL fee and tax reserve as separate terms. `cashNetSell = gross − fee − reserve` is a derived reconciliation value only and is never posted again. Output `withholding_kind` is derived from `tax_regime`; both kinds exclude the reserve from BUY funding, while only `self_reserved` remains in expected physical cash. |
| 6 | FX-buffer names drifted across prose and proposed contracts. | Freeze input `fx_buffer_rate` and monetary output `fx_buffer_amount`; the UI label is “Margine di sicurezza FX.” The buffer stays cash, is not a fee, and is not converted or subtracted twice. |
| 7 | Route-level rows risked fabricating or duplicating target/residual decisions. | Target notional, planned value, residual, and residual ratio are Asset-level values aggregated across all Brokers. | 8 | `Ora`, `Target`, and `Dopo` could use inconsistent denominators by adding cash to only one series. | All three category/exposure series use one denominator: invested Asset value in `valuation_currency`, with cash excluded. | 10 | Monetary SELL lacked a precise step/inventory rule and could hide a special “sell all” escape. | `monetary_amount` SELL must be a multiple of `order_amount_step`; `M_sell / P^(sell)` must not exceed exact custody. | 11 | Arbitrary Big-M constants could make linking constraints unsound or numerically opaque. | Derive every finite upper bound from normalized facts: reachable native cash and charge price/step for BUY, exact inventory and SELL price/step for SELL, and route-derived sums/ORs for Asset indicators. If a finite bound cannot be derived, return `needs_input` or `unsupported`; never inject an arbitrary Big-M. |
| 12 | A joint BUY/SELL optimum could discard the best invest-only result while claiming to implement the product's “use cash first” rule. | `invest_and_sell` is sequential: solve and freeze the best `invest_only` phase, then allow SELL of overweight holdings only to reduce the residual gap, applying turnover/cost/order-count tie-breaks. | 13 | Selected but unrouteable cash could inflate the PAC target denominator and make every allocation appear deficient. | `K_reachable` includes only gross cash that can reach at least one eligible BUY under declared routes/caps. ## Final v1 strategy shortlist

### Confirmed PAC strategies

1. **`proportional` — Base proportional.** Allocate toward target weights, quantize according to each allowed Broker, and leave the resulting residual visible.
2. **`min_fragmentation` — Minimum purchase fragmentation.** Do not invest less than the proportional baseline; first minimize Assets split across Brokers, then order rows, declared priorities, and fees, with a stable deterministic final tie-break.

Both respect hard routing, native cash, FX, fee, minimum-order, and exact Decimal objective constraints. Neither uses a hidden epsilon or may invent a recommendation outside the supplied target. PAC target and deviation use Asset-level totals over `K_reachable`; trapped cash is reported separately and excluded from that denominator.

### Confirmed Rebalancer strategies

1. **`invest_only` — Invest only.** Default; uses only selected cash/new liquidity and never sells implicitly.
2. **`invest_and_sell` — Invest and sell.** First solves and freezes the best invest-only plan, then permits sales of above-target holdings to reduce remaining gaps. It may not sell beyond custody or BUY and SELL the same Asset. Fractional SELL uses the same Broker/currency capability and amount step as fractional BUY and has no hidden sell-all exception. Only net proceeds after SELL fee and the estimated tax reserve may finance later BUY instructions. The exhaustive oracle must apply the same invest-only-then-sell sequence.

### Confirmed output comparison

When the declared domain is computable, the same run returns a base solution and a residual-optimized solution in the same shape. The second is additive and cannot rewrite the first. The artifact briefly conditions this on a reasonable benchmark, then later lists same-run delivery as frozen for `1.0.0`; the later frozen-assumption section is the current decision, while benchmark/limits remain implementation gates. If benchmarks contradict it, the decision must be reopened rather than silently changed.
