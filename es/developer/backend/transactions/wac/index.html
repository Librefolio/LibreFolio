    ⚖️ WAC & Cost Basis

    WAC (Weighted Average Cost) — also called PMC (Prezzo Medio di Carico) — is LibreFolio's blended, per-unit cost-basis method. It has one implementation: compute_average_costs() in backend/app/services/financial_math/average_cost.py. The Dashboard's purchase cost, realized sales and unrealized P&L, the Holdings table's average cost and Yield on Cost, the WAC lines of the lots analysis, the transaction preview, the automatic cost basis, the WAC series of POST /portfolio/lots/analysis and the PAC planner all read it.

    The cost it returns is historical: each acquisition costs what was actually paid, converted into the requested currency at the rate of its own date. It never moves with today's exchange rate.

    flowchart LR
    AC["compute_average_costs()<br/>financial_math/average_cost.py"]
    FX["fx.conversion.convert_bulk()"]
    Engine["PortfolioCalculationEngine<br/>DailyStateBuilder replay"]
    PS["PortfolioService<br/>summary · contribution · holdings"]
    Facade["compute_wac_iterative()<br/>facade"]
    Batch["Batch auto modes"]
    Pac["PAC planner"]
    Lots["LotsAnalysisService<br/>WAC lines"]

    AC -->|"one batched call"| FX
    PS --> Engine --> AC
    Batch --> Facade
    Pac --> Facade
    Facade --> AC
    Lots --> AC

    What it replaced

    The former WAC helper compute_wac_from_txlist(), the portfolio engine's own WAC pools (_buy_unit_cost(), _apply_split_rescale(), _compute_open_cost_basis_inline()) and TransactionService.get_cost_basis() no longer exist: compute_average_costs() in financial_math/average_cost.py replaced them all. The engine used to keep each pool in the asset currency and convert it at the valuation date, the lots analysis fell back to the unconverted cost when a rate was missing, and the former broker summary summed BUY amounts. They now share the rules below.

    🧱 The Financial Math Layer

    compute_average_costs() and compute_native_cash() live in backend/app/services/financial_math/, the one home of LibreFolio's financial calculations. The layer's rules, its package map and its guards are on their own page: Financial Math Layer.

    Module Calculation
    average_cost.py Average cost of positions: historical cost in the report and asset currencies, the pool after every movement, and the conversions that could not be made
    native_cash.py Native cash of every broker: balances in their own currencies, their value in the report currency on any day, the exchange result on cash, the period flows by type and position, and the conversions that could not be made

    Each module has one entry point: compute_average_costs() and compute_native_cash().

    Their tests are pure — no database, no server, convert_bulk replaced on the module with a double — and live in backend/test_scripts/test_services/test_financial_math/ (test_average_cost.py, test_native_cash.py), next to the layer's guard:

    ./dev.py test services financial-math

    🧮 The Average-Cost Function

    async def compute_average_costs(
    session: Any,
    positions: Sequence[CostPosition],
    *,
    report_currency: str,
    asset_leg: bool = True,
) -> dict[Hashable, AverageCost]:

    Below, T is report_currency and A is a position's asset currency.

    📥 Input: plain data

    Type Fields Meaning
    CostPosition key, asset_currency, movements One position. key is any hashable the caller chooses — (asset_id, broker_id), a broker id, a string; a duplicate key raises ValueError
    CostMovement movement_id, transaction_type, date, kind, quantity, cost_amount, cost_currency One movement of the position's quantity, in the order the caller recorded them

    CostMovementKind says what a movement does to the pool:

    Kind Quantity Cost
    ACQUISITION > 0 cost_amount is the total actually paid, in cost_currency; None means the cost is not known, 0 a free acquisition
    REDUCTION < 0 None: the pool decides what leaves
    SPLIT > 0 or < 0 None: a split moves quantity, never cost

    CostMovement rejects a zero quantity, an acquisition that removes quantity, a reduction that adds it, a cost on anything but an acquisition, and a cost without its currency.

    💱 Conversions

    The function converts each acquisition itself, at the acquisition's own date d:

    Leg Rate needed when Cost of the acquisition
    Report (T) paid currency ≠ T c_T = paid × r(paid→T, d). An amount already in T is taken as it is, so a purchase paid in the report currency never needs a rate
    Asset (A), only with asset_leg=True A ≠ T and paid currency ≠ A c_A = c_T × r(T→A, d), the same day's rate. Paid in A: c_A is the amount paid. A = T: c_A = c_T

    Since c_A and c_T describe the same purchase at the same day's rate, a position's exchange-rate effect is zero on the day it is bought.

    All the conversions of all the positions go to the FX service in one convert_bulk(..., raise_on_error=False) call, each requested once. When every amount is already in the currency it is needed in, no call is made and session is not used. convert_bulk backfills without limit (the latest rate on or before the date), so a conversion is missing only when the pair has no rate at all on or before that date, or no route.

    A missing rate never becomes a zero

    The acquisition's quantity still enters the pool, its cost does not: the pool is marked incomplete and the pair is reported as a MissingConversion with every date it was needed on. A report-leg gap is named FROM/TO (GBP/EUR for a purchase paid in pounds, T = EUR) with leg="report"; an asset-leg gap is named A/T (USD/EUR) with leg="asset", whichever direction the rate was requested in.

    🔁 Pool rules

    The fold keeps, for each position, the quantity Q and the total cost C on each leg:

    Movement Effect on the pool CostEffect
    Same-day ordering Movements that add quantity first, then those that remove it; within each group the caller's order is kept —
    Acquisition with a known cost Q += q, C += c ADD
    Acquisition at zero cost Q += q, C unchanged, completeness unchanged ADD_ZERO_COST
    Acquisition of unknown cost Q += q, C unchanged, pool incomplete on both legs; the id is listed in unknown_cost_movement_ids ADD_UNKNOWN_COST
    Acquisition whose rate is missing Q += q, C unchanged, pool incomplete on the report leg (and on the asset leg, unless paid in A); pair and date reported ADD_MISSING_FX
    Reduction Removes C × q / Q on both legs — the whole C when it empties the pool, so cost is conserved exactly. Quantity beyond the pool is clamped and the id is listed in oversold_movement_ids REDUCE
    Split Q changes, C is kept SPLIT_RESCALE
    Pool emptied C = 0 and the pool is complete again: a gap before a full closure does not follow later purchases —

    📤 Output

    compute_average_costs() returns one AverageCost per key:

    Field Meaning
    steps One CostStep per movement: the pool after it, in pool order
    missing MissingConversion(pair, dates, leg) for every conversion that could not be made
    unknown_cost_movement_ids Acquisitions with no known cost
    oversold_movement_ids Reductions larger than the pool (no caller surfaces them yet)
    quantity, cost_report, cost_asset, report_complete, asset_complete, unit_cost_report, unit_cost_asset, has_missing_report_fx Properties that read the last step
    state_at(day) The pool after the last movement dated on or before day; None before the first one
    step_for(movement_id) The step of one movement
    CostStep field Meaning
    movement, effect The movement and what it did to the pool
    quantity, cost_report, cost_asset The pool after the movement; cost_asset is None without the asset leg
    cost_report_change, cost_asset_change Cost added (positive) or removed (negative) by this movement
    report_complete, asset_complete False while part of the pool's cost is unknown on that leg
    conversion CostConversion of a converted acquisition: original amount and currency, converted amount, rate (converted / original), rate_date, days_back
    unit_cost_report, unit_cost_asset, cost_report_for(q), cost_asset_for(q) Per-unit cost, and the cost of q units of the pool

    Known part vs. complete cost

    While a pool is incomplete, cost_report holds only its known part: the unknown part counts at zero. Aggregates use it as it is — the portfolio's open cost basis includes it — while per-position figures (a WAC, an unrealized P&L, a point of a lots-analysis WAC line) are withheld rather than shown as a partial average.

    🔌 Adapters

    cost_movement_from_transaction(tx, *, asset_currency, split_linked, share=1) maps a Transaction to its movement:

    Transaction Movement
    Quantity None or 0 (also once scaled by share) None: not a movement
    Split-linked (ADJUSTMENT linked to a SPLIT AssetEvent) SPLIT, whatever its sign
    Quantity < 0, any type REDUCTION
    BUY ACQUISITION costing abs(amount) × share in its cash currency tx.currency (the asset currency when unset); no amount means a free acquisition
    Any other type adding quantity (TRANSFER, ADJUSTMENT) with cost_basis_override ACQUISITION costing cost_basis_override × quantity in cost_basis_currency (the asset currency when unset)
    The same without cost_basis_override ACQUISITION of unknown cost

    share — the owner's share of the broker — scales quantity and cost alike.

    determine_target_currency(movements, asset_currency) returns the currency of the most recent movement that adds quantity; on a date tie the first one in input order wins. A split, an acquisition of unknown cost, or no addition at all falls back to the asset currency.

    💶 The Native-Cash Function

    async def compute_native_cash(
    session: Any,
    movements: Sequence[CashMovement],
    *,
    report_currency: str,
    rates_until: date_type | None = None,
) -> NativeCash:

    financial_math/native_cash.py is the single reconstruction of every broker's cash. It keeps each broker's cash in the currencies it was recorded in, and the engine, the summary and the history endpoints all read it. Below, T is report_currency.

    📥 Movements and Shares

    Name Meaning
    CashMovement One change of a broker's cash, as stored: movement_id, transaction_type, date, broker_id, asset_id (None without a position), currency, the signed amount, and share, the part of the broker the caller counts (default 1). native is amount × share
    cash_movement_from_transaction(tx, *, share=1) The movement of a Transaction, or None when it records no amount or no currency. A zero amount is kept: it belongs to the broker's history but changes no balance
    effective_share(role, share_percentage) The share a user counts: an OWNER its share_percentage (None counts the whole broker, a zero share is kept), any other role — editor, viewer — the whole broker
    rates_until The last day the balances are valued on; None means each currency's last movement

    💱 Two Conversion Calls

    The conversions go to the FX service in two calls:

    • every non-zero movement in a currency other than T, at its own date — for the flows — in one convert_bulk(..., raise_on_error=False) call;
    • one unit of each foreign currency on every day from its first movement through rates_until — for the balances — in one unit_rate_steps() call (P3): the windows of days of each currency (_unit_windows()) give the same rates as a request a day, kept as the days a stored rate changes. NativeCash.unit_rates is a view on them (financial_math/fx_rates.AskedDayRates): a key answers on an asked day only, as the day-by-day map did, and _rate_at() and the calendar of the days a rate changes read the steps, never every day. The days start on the currency's first movement, even when it is centuries back (_unit_windows()): the rates are read as steps, so the cost follows the stored rate rows, not the calendar days. Before D-P4 (lot MF-PAV-L1) the days started no earlier than 1 January 1900, a floor against a movement stored centuries back asking one rate per day (R13-SEC8); it is gone.

    When every non-zero amount is already in T, no call is made and session is not used: cash held only in the report currency never needs a rate.

    Rule Effect
    Latest rate A balance is valued at the latest rate on or before the day, so foreign cash is revalued every day
    Zero amount Changes no figure and never needs a rate
    Share Scales every amount
    Missing conversion Never becomes a zero: the movement stays out of values and flows, its own-currency balance still counts it, and its pair is reported as MissingCashConversion(pair, dates) (USD/EUR for dollars with T = EUR) with every date it was needed on

    rates_until must reach every movement and every valued day

    The engine passes the run's end date, on or after every movement it reads. The function does not check it: a day after rates_until is valued at the last rate requested, and value_at() raises ValueError when a non-zero foreign balance has no rate at all on or before the day.

    📤 The NativeCash Result

    A movement is counted when its amount is not zero and its conversion was made. Windows are (date_from, date_to]; None leaves that side open.

    Member Returns
    value_at(day) Each broker's cash on day in T, every foreign balance at that day's rate. Only brokers with a counted movement on or before day appear
    balances(as_of=None) Each broker's balance in each currency, in that currency; zero and unconverted movements included
    flows(date_from, date_to) CashFlows, the window's movements at their own dates: signed_by_type, absolute_by_type, income_by_position and costs_by_position keyed (broker_id, asset_id), unallocated_income and unallocated_costs by broker. Income (DIVIDEND, INTEREST) keeps its sign, costs (FEE, TAX) are absolute
    position_flow_steps(date_from, date_to) Each position's income and costs of the window as running totals, (broker_id, asset_id) → [(day, income, costs)], one entry per day with such a movement, by the rules of flows(): one pass where a daily series would call flows() every day (#48). With the movements in date order, as the engine loads them, each entry equals flows(date_from, day) for the position exactly
    sums_by_date(date_from, date_to, transaction_types) Counted movements summed by date and type, dates ascending
    missing_conversions(date_from, date_to, transaction_types) One MissingCashConversion per pair
    exchange_results(date_from, date_to) Each foreign-cash broker's exchange result on cash, ascending by broker, zeros included; {} for an empty window
    rate_change_days(date_from, date_to) The days in [date_from, date_to] on which the rate of a held foreign currency changes; a currency's first rate counts. Found by change_days(), below
    foreign_cash_broker_ids Brokers with a counted movement outside T

    rate_change_days() takes its days from the module's pure function change_days(read, currencies, days), the one home of «the day a rate changes», which the portfolio engine calls too, each with its own reading: the native cash the last rate known on or before the day, on the calendar of its rate points, and the engine the exact day's rate in fx_rate_map, on every replayed day (see The Days the Engine Recomputes).

    The exchange result is what the rates did to the cash a broker held: the change in value of its foreign balances, each at its day's rate, less the window's foreign movements converted at their own dates.

    result(b) = Σ over c ≠ T of [ N(b, c, t1) × r(c, t1) − N(b, c, t0) × r(c, t0) ]
          − Σ over b's counted movements m in (t0, t1], currency ≠ T, of converted(m) × share(m)

    CONVERSION_DUST (1e-9): the same cash converted twice — at its movement's date and through the day's unit rate — can differ in the last of Decimal's 28 digits. exchange_results() returns the difference whole; a caller showing an exchange result leaves out anything smaller.

    🔌 Native-Cash Callers

    Caller Use
    PortfolioCalculationEngine.calculate() (financial_math/portfolio/engine.py, step 10c) Builds it once per run from the classified transactions dated up to the run's end, each at its broker's effective_share(), with rates_until = the run's end; hands it to DailyStateBuilder(native_cash=...) and exposes it as PortfolioCalculationResult.native_cash
    DailyStateBuilder._revalue_foreign_cash() (financial_math/portfolio/daily_state.py) On every rate_change_days() day of the replay, those before date_from included, after the day's cash movements and before its transactions: books value_at(day) minus the replayed cash of each foreign_cash_broker_ids broker into that broker's cash and R_b (a negative R_b drains into K_b, like a FEE), never into the capital baseline → Portfolio Engine — §6 Three-Pool Cash Model

    💱 Unrealized split by currency

    For the positions in assets priced in currency A, reported in T, with r(t) the A→T rate of the day and C_A, C_T their historical cost in A and in T, every daily state holds in unrealized_by_currency[A] the sum over those positions of:

    Part Formula Meaning
    asset MV − C_A × r(t) What the assets did in their own currency, at the day's rate
    fx C_A × r(t) − C_T What the exchange rate did to their cost
    unsplit MV − C_T Positions that cannot be split that day: no market value, no rate, or a cost incomplete on either leg

    For A = T the whole MV − C_T goes to asset: fx is always zero. A position valued at its book cost (ESTIMATED_AT_COST, no price at all) adds to no part (_add_unrealized_part(..., at_cost=True)): its MV − C_T is zero by construction, and splitting it would invent an asset part and an exchange-rate part of opposite sign; its currency keeps its entry. Summed over the currencies, the parts equal market_value − open_cost_basis exactly.

    PortfolioService turns two of these states into PortfolioSummary.period_unrealized_breakdown (_unrealized_breakdown_rows()): UnrealizedBreakdownRow(kind, asset_currency, period_delta) holds the change between the last state on or before date_from (zero without one) and the state at the end date — the same two states as period_unrealized_gain_loss_delta, so the rows add up to it exactly. Order: an asset row for every currency (the report currency first, then alphabetical), an fx row for every other currency, then an unsplit row only for a currency whose unsplit part is non-zero at either boundary. The list is empty when there is no unrealized delta.

    🧾 The WAC Facade

    compute_wac_iterative() in backend/app/services/portfolio_service.py serves the callers that need one position's WAC with its qualifying rows. Its contract did not change with the single implementation.

    async def compute_wac_iterative(
    session: AsyncSession,
    broker_id: int,
    asset_id: int,
    as_of_date: date,
    asset_currency: str,
    excluded_tx_ids: list[int] | None = None,
    target_currency_override: str | None = None,
    use_cache: bool = True,
) -> WACPreviewResultItem:

    The facade:

    1. queries the non-zero-quantity transactions of the broker and asset through as_of_date;
    2. identifies the ADJUSTMENT rows linked to a SPLIT AssetEvent;
    3. fingerprints the rows (id and updated_at) and the split linkage for the WAC cache, keyed together with the broker, asset, date, asset currency, override and excluded ids; use_cache=False bypasses the cache;
    4. drops the excluded ids and sorts the rest by (date, id); with no rows left it returns a zero WAC in the asset currency — which the batch's Auto step refuses (see Nothing to average);
    5. maps the rows with cost_movement_from_transaction();
    6. chooses the target currency: target_currency_override, or determine_target_currency() — the currency of the last acquisition;
    7. calls compute_average_costs() for that one position, with asset_leg=False;
    8. returns wac=None, no qualifying rows and the missing pairs with their dates when a report-leg conversion is missing — that result is not cached;
    9. otherwise returns the WAC (unit_cost_report, in the target currency, rounded to what Transaction.cost_basis_override keeps with round_computed_value() — C-97 decision D6, MF-W56: the preview is frozen into a manual cost and the auto cost is written on the row, so a periodic quotient must fit the column; the average itself stays exact in average_cost) and one WACQualifyingTX per step, and caches the result.

    Each step becomes a qualifying row:

    CostEffect WACQualifyingTX.effect unit_cost
    ADD add Cost added ÷ quantity, in the target currency
    ADD_ZERO_COST add_zero_cost 0
    ADD_UNKNOWN_COST add_zero_cost 0: the preview counts an unknown cost at zero
    REDUCE reduce The running WAC before the reduction
    SPLIT_RESCALE split_rescale The running WAC after the split

    running_wac is the WAC after the step. A converted acquisition also carries its provenance: fx_info (rate date and days back), original_unit_cost, original_currency and fx_rate_used.

    Caller as_of_date Target currency
    Batch auto modes (TransactionService._compute_wac_for_auto_items()) The row's date; the partner's date for the receiving side of a TRANSFER. The batch costs its Auto rows in the order of this date (see Inline Batch Auto Modes) The cost_basis_override.code sent as a hint, or the last acquisition's currency
    PAC planner (portfolio_allocation_source.py) The planner's date The planner's target currency, as override, with use_cache=False

    Two target-currency rules

    The documented decision WAC target currency = last acquisition's currency (devWiki: decisions/wac-target-currency-last-acquisition) still holds for the transaction preview and the automatic cost basis. The Dashboard and everything the portfolio engine produces use the report currency.

    🧾 Persisted Cost Basis

    transactions.cost_basis_override is a per-unit amount, not a total acquisition cost. Its currency is stored in cost_basis_currency; the two values are written and cleared together by the transaction flows.

    Field Meaning
    cost_basis_override Frozen WAC for one unit
    cost_basis_currency ISO currency code for that per-unit amount

    A BUY costs the absolute cash amount, in its cash currency. A TRANSFER or ADJUSTMENT adding quantity costs cost_basis_override × quantity, in cost_basis_currency, converted at its own date like any other acquisition. A BUY without amount is a free acquisition; a TRANSFER or ADJUSTMENT without override has an unknown cost (see Diagnostics). Which rows need an override is read from TX_TYPE_METADATA, the table GET /transactions/types serves: TransactionService._requires_cost_basis() calls cost_basis_required() (backend/app/schemas/transactions/type_metadata.py), which takes the type's cost_basis_pair when it has one — [1] for a positive quantity, the receiving side, [0] otherwise — and its cost_basis_mode if not; required_qty_pos asks for an override when the quantity is positive.

    Rule 12 of validate_transaction_business_rules() (backend/app/schemas/transactions/transactions.py) reads the same table through cost_basis_allowed(): a row sent with a cost_basis_mode is refused (costBasisModeIncompatible) when the rule of its side — picked at call time by _cost_basis_rule(), shared with cost_basis_required() — is forbidden, or required_qty_pos without a positive quantity. Rule 12b (W-B72, night decision N78) holds to the same test the non-null cost_basis_override a create or an update sets without a mode — with one, rule 12 speaks alone — and refuses it with costBasisNotAllowed, params {type, qty}. That includes an ADJUSTMENT taking units out: it has no value (N78-1; see Split & Promote). Rule 12b never judges a cost basis the row already stores, nor an explicit null; before W-B72, a cost basis sent without a mode was stored on any type (see Per-type rules, and Promote boundary for the cost basis a promote resolves). cost_basis_allowed() is the test the frontend makes before sending a cost basis (cbAllowed in frontend/src/lib/features/transactions/txPayloadHelpers.ts), and with today's table it admits exactly the rows that need an override: a TRANSFER or ADJUSTMENT adding quantity.

    The batch rejects such a row without an override (costBasisRequired) when it is created or made the receiving side of a promoted pair (see Promote boundary), and when an update changes its type, quantity, cost basis or its asset event link, or names a cost_basis_mode; a row in Auto is given the computed WAC instead. The update check, _check_update_cost_basis(), compares the row's cost_fields() — type, quantity, cost_basis_override, cost_basis_currency, asset_event_id — with stored_cost_fields, which _load_existing_transactions() takes when the batch loads its rows. So an update of only the notes, tags or date of a row stored without an override passes, and the row keeps its unknown cost. An acquisition of unknown cost therefore comes from:

    • data the batch never checked: written outside it, or promoted by an earlier version, which did not check promoted pairs; or
    • an ADJUSTMENT linked to a SPLIT event retyped before release 1.3. Auto stores no cost on a split-linked row (see Split-linked adjustment skip), so without the split its units would have an unknown cost. Since P-10 the app refuses to take the split away while such a row has no cost: the retype answers 409 EVENT_SPLIT_ADJUSTMENTS_WITHOUT_COST, and new parameters for a Scheduled Investment plan, or leaving the plan for another provider, 409 PROVIDER_WIPE_SPLIT_ADJUSTMENTS_WITHOUT_COST when the split is one the plan generated (see Parametric Provider Change); once the row has a cost, 0 included, both go through (test_forward_split_whose_adjustment_has_no_cost_is_retyped_only_once_the_adjustment_has_one in backend/test_scripts/test_api/test_lots_wac_history.py for the retype; test_provider_wipe_split_guard.py in backend/test_scripts/test_services/ for the provider change). The market-data wipe of a currency change needs no such refusal since N68: it never deletes a SPLIT, so the adjustments keep their link and their cost whatever it was (test_market_data_wipe_keeps_splits.py, same folder; see Market-Data Wipe). Removing the provider altogether never takes such a split away: No Provider (DELETE /api/v1/assets/provider) answers the same 409 PROVIDER_WIPE_SPLIT_ADJUSTMENTS_WITHOUT_COST while a split the provider generated has such an adjustment (see Provider Removal; test_provider_removal_link_guard.py, in the same folder). Any other transaction linked to an event the provider generated is only disconnected (asset_event_id to NULL) and kept; the earlier refusal for every linked transaction (X-R62) is gone. The asset form counts what a change or removal deletes before it is sent, with the dry run POST /assets/provider/impact.

    An explicit null in an update (decision P5, coordinator reading B, 16/10). An update that leaves out cost_basis_override changes nothing. One that sends it as null removes the cost basis stored on the row — cost_basis_override and cost_basis_currency together — where the row, with the update's type and quantity applied, takes one (cost_basis_allowed()): _apply_update_values() (batch_edits.py) tells the null from a missing key by model_fields_set. Where a cost basis is required — on today's table, every side that takes one: a received TRANSFER, an ADJUSTMENT adding quantity — the removal changes the row's cost_fields(), so the update check above refuses it with costBasisRequired (operation update, field cost_basis_override), and the stored cost stays. Elsewhere the null is ignored. It is ignored too when the update names cost_basis_mode auto or auto-detail, which compute their own (see Inline Batch Auto Modes): the frontend sends a null with an Auto row that has no currency hint yet (buildUpdateDiff() in frontend/src/lib/features/transactions/txPayloadHelpers.ts). Tests: ./dev.py test services transaction-cost-basis-rule (TestDecisionP5AnExplicitNullEmptiesTheCost).

    🔄 Inline Batch Auto Modes

    cost_basis_mode is a request-only instruction on TXCreateItem and TXUpdateItem; it is not a database column.

    Mode Batch behavior
    auto Compute and persist the per-unit WAC; return a compact WAC result
    auto-detail Compute the same value and also return qualifying transactions and price detail
    manual Use the submitted cost_basis_override; do not add the row to the auto-WAC worklist

    The ordered batch flow is:

    create → promote → link resolution → compute_wac_and_fx_issues()
       → validate required cost basis → balance replay

    compute_wac_and_fx_issues() calls TransactionService._compute_wac_for_auto_items() only when no non-balance issue already exists. That helper scans only parsed_creates and parsed_updates for auto or auto-detail, and costs each item on its own row: a create on the row apply_creates() flushed for it, read by create index from context.created_by_index (its created_rows argument), and an update on the row its id names. It costs the rows in the order of their pool's date — the as_of date _auto_cost_source() returns, below — and on one date in the order they came: the creates by index, then the updates by index.

    For each selected row:

    • _auto_cost_source() chooses the pool to average. The receiving side of a TRANSFER — a TRANSFER row with related_transaction_id — averages its partner's broker as of the partner's date, when the units left, leaving out the partner's outgoing leg and the row itself;
    • any other row — an incoming ADJUSTMENT — averages its own broker as of its own date, leaving itself out;
    • the batch's other Auto rows whose cost is not written yet — split-linked ones aside — are left out too: their ids join the excluded ids that _auto_cost() passes to compute_wac_iterative() (decision N84, below);
    • _auto_cost() averages that pool with compute_wac_iterative(), once it has checked that every row of it can be read (see Unreadable pool);
    • a supplied cost_basis_override.code acts as the target-currency hint; and
    • a calculated wac is written to cost_basis_override and cost_basis_currency on the staged ORM row.

    related_transaction_id is in place before the WAC step whatever the origin of the pair: resolve_create_links() sets it for a pair created in the same batch, apply_promotes() for a pair formed by a promote, and a saved pair being updated brings it from the database (TXUpdateItem has no link_uuid). Since cost_basis_mode is accepted only on a TRANSFER or ADJUSTMENT adding quantity (costBasisModeIncompatible otherwise; see Persisted Cost Basis), a TRANSFER in Auto is always the receiving side.

    Reading the source pool as it was when the units left keeps their cost: a transfer of the whole position keeps the source's average, where counting the outgoing leg would empty the pool and give 0; a partial transfer gets the same value with or without that leg, since a reduction does not move the unit cost; and purchases made on the source broker while the units are in transit stay out.

    The order and the exclusion make one rule (W-B67, decision N84 of the coordinator, 17/10): each Auto row gets the cost it would get were the rows saved one at a time in date order. Three cases follow from it:

    • a split-linked ADJUSTMENT in Auto is never left out: it only rescales the pool, and it gets no cost (see Split-linked adjustment skip);
    • a row whose cost could not be written — an unreadable pool, a missing conversion, a pool with nothing to average — stays out of the later pools, never counted at zero, and the batch refuses it anyway (autoCostUnreadable, wacFxUnavailable, autoCostEmptyPool, autoCostUnknownPool); and
    • an update in Auto counts as not costed yet: the cost its row holds is the one the Auto step replaces.

    wac_results keeps the order of the items — the creates, then the updates — not the order they were costed in, and each item carries the value written on its own row. The excluded ids are part of the facade's cache key (see The WAC Facade), so no Auto row reads a result cached for another's pool: a cost the batch writes changes no row's updated_at, and the fingerprint alone would not tell the pools apart.

    Before W-B67, a create in Auto without a link_uuid was found as the newest row with its broker, asset, date and type (_find_created_tx_for_wac(), since removed). Of two Auto rows sharing those four fields, the first kept no cost — the batch's cost check skips Auto creates, so it committed without one — and the second got it twice; an Auto row followed by a Manual one sharing them wrote its average over the Manual cost. The rows were also costed by index, each with the others in its pool as they stood — a create not yet costed at zero cost, as an acquisition of unknown cost (ADD_UNKNOWN_COST) or at the 0 of its currency hint — so the result followed the order of the indexes. With 10 units bought at 100 EUR and two Auto ADJUSTMENTs of +5 on the same later day, the first row got no cost and the second ≈ 66.67 (1000 / 15); fixing the row lookup alone would have given ≈ 66.67 and ≈ 88.89; now both get 100. Tests: ./dev.py test services transaction-cost-basis-rule WB67 (TestWB67EachAutoCreateCostsItsOwnRow, TestWB67AutoRowsAreCostedInDateOrder, TestWB67GuardsWhereTodaysSearchFindsTheRow).

    The giving leg of each TRANSFER pair the batch splits takes no cost: it becomes an ADJUSTMENT without a value (decision 47 as N78-1 changed it; see Split & Promote), and so does a giving leg a broker deletion leaves without its partner (see Broker Deletion).

    The create stage has already flushed new rows for generated IDs, and all of this work remains in the caller's session. Neither the WAC helper nor the surrounding batch stages commit.

    🧬 Split-linked adjustment skip

    If the selected row references an AssetEvent of type SPLIT, auto mode clears both stored cost-basis fields and emits a batch WAC result with wac=None and no missing pairs. The average cost rescales the live pool when it meets the split-linked adjustment; storing the pre-split WAC would double-count the economic cost.

    💱 Missing FX behavior

    If compute_wac_iterative() returns no WAC and reports missing FX pairs, the batch stage appends a TXValidationIssue with:

    • code wacFxUnavailable;
    • the originating create/update operation and index;
    • field cost_basis_override; and
    • pair names plus required dates in params.

    The response may still include wac_results, but the issue makes committed=False; the route that owns the session then rolls back.

    🚫 Unreadable pool

    Since W-B58, a stored decimal that is no number — a text, or a REAL ±inf that an older version or another tool left — reads back as None instead of raising (RealDecimal, see Decimal Columns). The facade does not stop on such a row: cost_movement_from_transaction() skips a row whose quantity reads None, takes a BUY whose amount reads None for a free purchase, and an acquisition whose cost basis reads None for one of unknown cost, counted at zero. An Auto cost averaged from that pool would be written on the row, wrong for good.

    So _auto_cost() (auto_cost.py, W-B69) first counts the unreadable rows of the pool with _unreadable_pool_rows() — through count_unreadable_rows() (unreadable_rows.py), the count the balance replay makes too (W-B74): the rows compute_wac_iterative() loads — same broker and asset, a date up to the pool's date, a quantity not NULL and not zero — minus the excluded ids, whose quantity or amount reads None, or whose cost_basis_override is stored (not NULL in SQL) but reads None. A NULL cost basis is a row without one, such as a BUY, and is not counted. When there is any, _auto_cost() raises UnreadablePoolError(count) before averaging. The error carries the count only, never a transaction id: the pool can be another user's broker, since the receiving side of a TRANSFER averages its partner's.

    The guard is _auto_cost()'s, so it covers the costs written on rows — Auto rows, and the Auto cost a broker deletion writes on a received leg; the PAC planner call compute_wac_iterative() directly, without it. Where it raises, nothing averaged from that pool is written:

    Caller On UnreadablePoolError
    _compute_wac_for_auto_items(..., unreadable=None) With a list passed as unreadable, the item gets no cost and no WACPreviewResultItem, (operation, index, count) is appended to the list, and the other items go on. Without a list, the error raises
    delete_by_broker() The deletion of that broker fails whole (see Broker Deletion)

    compute_wac_and_fx_issues() (batch_costs.py) passes a list: each item in it becomes the issue autoCostUnreadable on its row — field cost_basis_override, params {"count": N}, no transaction id, since the pool can be another user's. The batch is not saved.

    🈳 Nothing to average

    An average of 0 is not always a cost. The facade gives 0 for a pool that holds no units on its date — the broker never held the asset, or sold all of it before, and the engine resets a pool that empties — and for a pool whose units all entered at an unknown cost, which the engine counts at zero (ADD_UNKNOWN_COST) while marking the cost incomplete. Written on an incoming row, either 0 would make it a free acquisition, silently: the form starts in Auto.

    So when the average it gets is 0, _compute_wac_for_auto_items() asks _nothing_to_average() why (N88, J-57). It reads the engine's own state through wac_pool_holding() (portfolio_service.py): the quantity and the cost completeness of the pool compute_wac_iterative() averaged, same rows, same exclusions, uncached. Holding no units, the item is refused with autoCostEmptyPool; holding units none of which has a known cost, with autoCostUnknownPool. A pool of free units — units at a known cost of 0 — holds units with a complete cost: its average of 0 stands.

    A refused item gets no cost and no WACPreviewResultItem; (operation, index, code) is appended to the list passed as nothing_to_average (without a list, NothingToAverageError raises), and compute_wac_and_fx_issues() turns each into the issue code on its row — field cost_basis_override, no params, no transaction id, since the pool can be another user's. The batch is not saved. The user can still enter 0 by hand, for units that really came free. Only the batch's Auto rows are checked: delete_by_broker() and the PAC planner keep the facade's 0. A pool whose cost is only partly unknown gives an understated average, written as before (J-58, a decision of the developer). Tests: ./dev.py test services transaction-cost-basis-rule N88.

    🔗 Promote boundary

    TXPromoteBatchItem has no cost_basis_mode, and apply_promotes() no longer performs an automatic WAC calculation. A promote-specific resolved value arrives as resolved_fields.cost_basis_override from an API client: the merge dialogs send description and tags only (see Request shape). The promote stage applies it to the positive-quantity transfer leg and clears it from the sender; without that key, the receiver keeps the cost basis it already has. Either way the sender ends without one: before it applies resolved_fields, apply_promotes() clears cost_basis_override and cost_basis_currency on a side whose new type takes none (cost_basis_allowed()), such as an ADJUSTMENT taking units out that an older version left with a cost (decision 47). A cash pair (CASH_TRANSFER, FX_CONVERSION) has no leg that takes one: a non-null value refuses the promote with costBasisNotAllowed (_resolved_cost_issues(), W-B72), where it was written on both legs before; a null passes.

    Rows that are also present in creates[] or updates[] enter auto-WAC only because that create/update item requested an auto mode, never because of the promote item itself.

    validate_cost_basis() checks the receiving side of a promoted pair like any other row:

    • a new row is checked as a create, unless it is in Auto: costBasisRequired on operation create, with its index;
    • a saved row also present in updates[] is checked as that update, unless it is in Auto: the promote changes its type, so even an update of its notes alone is checked; and
    • any other saved row without a cost basis gets costBasisRequired on operation promote, with the promote's index and ref_id set to that row (_check_promoted_saved_cost_basis()).

    🔍 Preview and Response Data

    The committed WAC series is read through POST /portfolio/lots/analysis, by asking for the BROKER_WAC_HISTORY component (one BrokerWACHistoryPoint per step and broker: wac and pool_qty) and the CUMULATIVE_WAC_HISTORY component (one CumulativeWACHistoryPoint per step across the requested brokers); see Lots Analysis Service. There is no stand-alone WAC route.

    Inline batch results use the same WACPreviewResultItem schema and additionally populate operation, index, and source_broker_id. The main fields are:
