    🧮 Financial Math Layer

    Every financial calculation in LibreFolio — purchase cost, P&L, capital, NAV, returns, FX value, yields — has exactly one implementation, and its home is backend/app/services/financial_math/. The services, AI Export, the risk engine and the PAC planner call this layer; the frontend shows the fields it computes.

    This page is for developers who add or change a calculation: the rule, the package map, the boundaries of the layer, how to add a calculation, and the guards that keep each calculation in one place. The details of each engine are on the pages listed under Deep Pages.

    Where the rule is written

    The rule is decision 10 of .github/copilot-instructions.md (developer decision of 2026-10-11). Two instruction files carry it for coding agents: .github/instructions/backend.instructions.md, section «Financial Math — One Home», maps where each calculation lives; .github/instructions/financial-math.instructions.md, loaded for every file of the layer, holds its rules and its guards.

    🎯 The Rule

    1. Search before writing. Before computing anything financial, look in financial_math/. If the calculation exists, call it — with parameters when your case differs — instead of deriving it again.
    2. Add there. A new calculation goes into financial_math/, next to the ones it relates to, with its unit tests.
    3. No local or partial copies. A loop over transactions that recomputes cost, capital, NAV or P&L outside these engines is a defect, even when it serves a single card or export: read the engine's output instead.
    4. Report copies. Report any copy you meet, old or new. Before closing work that touched financial math, search the project for copies of what you changed.

    The technical indicators (services/signal_plugins/, services/signals/runtime.py), the risk engine (services/risk/) and the PAC allocator (services/pac_allocator/) stay where they are, but take the common math — returns, annualization, conversions, average cost, valuation — from the layer. FX conversion itself stays in the FX service: convert_bulk() in services/fx/conversion.py is the primitive the calculations call, and unit_rate_steps(), in the same module, gives the rates of long ranges as steps (P3).

    🪞 Declared Float Twins

    Four float functions of backend/app/services/risk/metrics.py have a counterpart in returns.py. They are declared twins, not copies, and they stay in risk/metrics.py on purpose: series statistics live there (.github/instructions/backend-risk.instructions.md). The layer's versions take and return Decimal: NAV snapshots and cash flows for the TWRR, a rate over a span of days for the conversions. The twins run on the float return series of the risk analytics.

    • compounded_return(returns) — Π(1 + r) − 1, the geometric chaining that calculate_twrr() applies to its sub-period returns.
    • wealth_index(returns) — 1, then Π(1 + r) after each return: the float form of the wealth_index of calculate_twrr_period_series().
    • period_returns_from_cumulative(cumulative_returns) — (1 + cₜ) / (1 + cₜ₋₁) − 1, which takes a cumulative return series back to period returns: the inverse of chaining the TWRR.
    • daily_risk_free_rate(annual_rate, periods_per_year) — expm1(log1p(R) / f), an annual rate per observation, the same compounding as annualized_to_cumulative() and cumulative_to_annualized().

    fifo/models.py also holds the engine's economic allocations (events, groups, per-lot accumulators); portfolio/models.py holds ValuationSource, EngineEndState and PortfolioCalculationResult.

    period_mwrr() and period_mwrr_series() (returns.py) hold the Dashboard's MWRR rule, one rule for the card and for every point of the line (developer decision D-MF5): no rate when the value is zero or less, or when the net invested capital up to that day — the first snapshot's value plus the flows after it (decision N54) — is zero or less; the series' last point is the card. calculate_mwrr() and calculate_mwrr_series() remain the bare solves, without that rule, and every point of the series is the single solve ending on its snapshot (one starting guess). A series gives each point the rate of that single solve bit for bit, and is faster on long histories (P1, N74): when the cold Newton step from the starting guess fails, it skips the grid scan only where the scan's answer is known — the previous point's rate, by Newton, points at the one true root (one sign change in the flows, Descartes' rule), and the answer is the scan's own Brent call on that grid interval (_mwrr_solve, _bracketed_xirr_at); everywhere else the scan runs. A rate is returned only when it solves the equation: its NPV within 1e-7 of the flows' size (_is_xirr_root, the test of Newton's answer); a root the bracketing found may instead show the NPV changing sign within ±1e-9 of it (_is_bracketed_xirr_root), which keeps the true root of an ill-conditioned NPV, whose terms are far larger than the flows and cancel at the root (P1b). A sign change is read from the two signs, never from their product, which underflows to 0.0 when both NPVs are tiny; and a sign is trusted only above the NPV's rounding noise where its terms underflow (_npv_noise_floor, _opposite_signs). A grid point whose NPV is exactly 0.0 counts as a root only where the NPV changes sign around it (_changes_sign_at, in the scan and in _bracketed_xirr_at; P1c): on a period that opens at 0 and whose flows all lie decades after its start (a typo dated in 1900), every term underflows to 0.0 at the top of the grid, and that point solved nothing; the period has no rate there («—»), while a genuine exact zero, a rate of exactly 0, still changes sign and counts. Flows that are all zero have no rate either (MF-P1d): the NPV is zero at every rate, and Newton would answer its own starting point (0.1, «10 %») — a period opening at 0 whose only deposit falls on its last day is one, since the deposit joins the final value (_all_flows_zero, in _solve_xirr and _newton_xirr).

    period_twrr() and period_twrr_series() (returns.py, MF-K3) hold the Dashboard's TWRR rule the same way, one rule for the card and every point of the line: period_twrr_series() is calculate_twrr_series() with None (PeriodTWRRPoint.twrr) wherever the snapshot's NAV is zero or less — an emptied portfolio has no time-weighted return, as it has no MWRR. The chain keeps compounding through those points (a sub-period starting from 0 returns 0), so after a refill the line shows its TWRR again; period_twrr() is the series' last point, the card. Empty without two snapshots or without flows. Before, the card had its own guard (engine_nav > 0) while the line stayed flat at its last value. calculate_twrr() and calculate_twrr_series() remain the bare chains, without that rule.

    period_roi_cash_flows(nav_snapshots, cash_flows, date_from) (returns.py) gives the flows the period's simple ROI counts its capital from, card and line alike: the period's flows (rebase_to_period()), except when date_from precedes every snapshot. The rebase leaves out the first day's flows there — they are inside the first NAV, which the TWRR and the MWRR start from — and the ROI would see no capital (0 % before MF-K3); that period holds the whole history, so its capital is every flow, as with no date_from. DerivedViewsBuilder.build_roi_cash_flows() calls it on the period's inputs, and reads the whole history's when date_from falls before the first state.

    marked_price() (portfolio/daily_state.py, MF6-1) is the one rule of «an asset's mark on a day» as the portfolio values it: the mark of the asset's AssetPriceSeries, a trade mark restated to current units through the splits since it was observed, in its own currency (MarkedPrice). DailyStateBuilder._market_value_for() values every holding with it, and the engine's result carries the same rule for its readers: PortfolioCalculationResult.marks, an AssetMarks (None when calculate() returns early) holding the replay's mark series, splits, quote_base_quantity and rates. price_at(asset_id, day) is the mark the valuation read; last_market_price_at(asset_id, day) the last market quote on or before day (a later trade observation does not move it); in_target(amount, currency, day) converts at day's rate, as the valuation converts a mark. It answers on the engine's days only, first_day … last_day, since its rates are the ones the engine asked (AskedDayRates): any other day is None, in any currency. portfolio_service reads the holdings' unit prices and their one-day changes (price_change_1d, gain_loss_change_1d, gain_loss_change_1d_percent) through it and has no price query or conversion of its own any more: the day before's price is last_market_price_at(day − 1) converted at the rate of day − 1 — not at the rate of the quote's own date — so a foreign quote that did not move shows only that day's rate move; on the replay's first day there is none.

    float_price_return() and observed_annualization() (returns.py) serve the float return series that the risk and signal statistics read. market_series.py, which prepares those series, takes both from here and keeps no return or annualization formula of its own.

    • float_price_return(start, end) is price_return() as a float. It raises ValueError on a missing price or a zero start: a series never carries an unavailable return.
    • observed_annualization(n_observations, baseline_date, final_date) returns the series' calendar span and its observed annualization factor, observations × 365 / days. An empty series gives (0, None); a span of zero days or less raises ValueError. A caller that can meet a zero-day span picks its own fallback before calling: portfolio_twrr_returns() (risk/results.py) leaves the factor None, the comparison analytic takes the context's factor.
    • observations_per_year(n_observations, calendar_days) is the formula itself, the one home that observed_annualization() computes with and that the risk contracts check a factor against: the validators of schemas/risk.py import it (MF-D3), the one kind of import a schema may make from the layer — a pure function, never a service (rule 8 of .github/instructions/financial-math.instructions.md).

    fixed_period_dates() and fixed_period_changes() (returns.py) hold the change of a price or rate over the fixed periods of the Assets and FX tables (developer decision D-MF6 b). FIXED_PERIODS lists their keys and calendar days, in display order: 1D 1, 1W 7, 1M 30, 3M 91, 6M 182, 1Y 365, 2Y 730, 3Y 1095, 5Y 1825. fixed_period_dates(as_of) names the days to value: as_of, then as_of − days for each period. The caller values those days: the last stored price on or before each (POST /assets/prices/period-changes), or the rate convert_bulk() gives on it (POST /fx/currencies/period-changes). fixed_period_changes(values, as_of) then gives each period both orientations, as fractions: change_ratio, value(as_of) / value(as_of − days) − 1, and inverse_change_ratio, value(as_of − days) / value(as_of) − 1, the change of the inverted quote. Each period is the house's range_change() (below) between the two values: None when either is missing, the earlier one ≤ 0 or the one on as_of negative, and −1 (−100 %) with no inverse when the value on as_of is exactly 0.

    range_change(reference, anchor) (returns.py) holds the change of a price or rate over the whole range a page shows, from its first point to its last (X-R59, decision N80): the whole-range Δ of the Assets cards and the Δ Abs / Δ % of the FX pages. The browser no longer computes them: the web app reads the endpoints' range_change (assetRangeDelta() and fxRangeDeltas() in frontend/src/lib/utils/rangeChange.ts); the asset detail page reads it from POST /assets/prices/period-changes for its stored series and POST /assets/prices/current for a live quote. The function returns a frozen RangeChange of unrounded Decimals: change_abs, anchor − reference; change_ratio, anchor / reference − 1; and the same for the inverted quote (an FX pair read the other way round), inverse_change_abs, 1 / anchor − 1 / reference, and inverse_change_ratio, reference / anchor − 1. All four are None when either value is missing, the reference ≤ 0 or the anchor < 0. An anchor of exactly 0 over a positive reference is a price written down to nothing (a defaulted loan, a delisted share): change_abs is −reference and change_ratio −1 (−100 %), as the Assets cards showed it, while the inverse fields stay None (X's review of X-R59). The period columns showed «—» for it and now show −100 % too. An FX pair never meets it: convert_bulk() skips a stored rate of 0. The same two endpoints value the ends, for an item that sends a range_start: the anchor is the value on as_of, as for the periods; the reference is the value on range_start (backward-filled), or, when there is none on or before it, the first value inside the range — the first point of the page's series. POST /assets/prices/current?range_start=… applies the same function to the current price, live or stored, as the anchor, for an asset page whose range ends on a live quote (Current Quotes and Today's OHLC).

    ⚙️ The Portfolio Package at Work

    The portfolio engine has no developer page of its own: its cost side is in WAC & Cost Basis, its valuation in Price Resolver, and its model in the theory page Portfolio Engine — Mathematical Model. One call of PortfolioCalculationEngine.calculate() (portfolio/engine.py):

    1. resolves the target currency (by default get_effective_base_currency()) and the broker scope, each broker at the share native_cash.broker_shares() gives it — its effective_share() (F2), or the whole broker with apply_ownership_share=False (the whole broker);
    2. loads the scope's transactions and their split links (load_split_links(), splits.py), and classifies them (ScopeAwareTransactionClassifier, classifier.py). A split whose stored ratio fails is_valid_split_ratio() (missing, not finite, or not above 0) counts as missing, never an exception (lot MF-D4; before, a ValueError). Its linked rows stay in split_linked_tx_ids, the split's rescale with their cost kept. Only the ratio is dropped: the event adds no SplitRecord to split_history, and goes once into PortfolioCalculationResult.invalid_split_events (InvalidSplitEvent, sorted by event id), which the data-quality report turns into INVALID_SPLIT_RATIO. The range of daily states it computes starts no earlier than the first transaction and ends no later than latest_range_end() (tomorrow, utils/datetime_utils.py): the replay walks every day, and an end far in the future held the instance (R13-SEC7-1). It has no date floor at the start: actual_from is max(date_from, first_tx_date), so a transaction dated before 1900, year 1 included, starts the states on its own day. The cost follows the engine's segments — the days something changes (see The Days the Engine Recomputes) — not the calendar days. A range that ends before the first transaction gives an empty result;
    3. returns the cached PortfolioCalculationResult when the portfolio_blob cache holds one for the same key — scope, currency, options, data fingerprints and the day of position_states_start — that starts on or before the requested start and ends on the requested end date;
    4. loads the prices, quote_base_quantity, currencies, types and classifications of the held assets, and preloads the FX rates in one unit_rate_steps() call (_preload_fx_rates(): each currency's windows of days, read as steps; the map answers on the asked days only, P3);
    5. computes the average cost of every position (compute_average_costs() over build_cost_positions()), one AssetPriceSeries per held asset (build_asset_price_series()) and the native cash (compute_native_cash());
    6. runs DailyStateBuilder (daily_state.py) in a worker thread, off the event loop, and stores its result in the cache.

    position_states_start holds the positions at the end of calculate(period_start=…) — the caller's period start, while the daily states run from date_from (t=0 for the Dashboard) — or of date_from when it is not named. internal_asset_transfers lists each linked TRANSFER of a security between two brokers of the scope, with its value on arrival and the cost the sender moved: the Performance table's transfer split (contributions.py, developer decision P22).

    How a closed row closed (MF-CB). A row closed at the period's end (is_fully_sold) says how: closed_by is the type of the movement that last took its quantity, at the run's share, to the dust threshold or below (holding_window.closing_move, the same close closed_position_window dates; a split never closes) — SELL, TRANSFER or ADJUSTMENT. A TRANSFER names the broker its units went to, closed_to_broker_id: the paired leg's (both legs carry related_transaction_id), when the user can read that broker, asked for or not; an orphan leg or another user's broker names none. So the Performance panel tells a sale from a move: a position moved whole from A to a selected B is held at B, not sold at A. Example: 10 units, 6 moved to B then 4 sold → SELL; 4 sold then 6 moved → TRANSFER to B. No figure changes. Tests: test_closing_move.py (test_financial_math/), test_closed_by.py (services closed-by).

    Callers derive their views from the result with DerivedViewsBuilder (derived_views.py) — history, per-broker P&L, candles, acquisition funding, performance inputs, allocation — and the data-quality report with build_data_quality_report() (data_quality.py).

    The daily states always start at t=0, so one cached result serves every window; the views take the window and build only its days (lot P2). build_history(), build_broker_pnl_history() and build_pnl_candles() cover [date_from, date_to], build_acquisition_funding() (date_from, date_to] (a flow series: a BUY dated date_from belongs to the period before), both found by bisection on the states' dates. build_performance_inputs(date_from) returns only what rebase_to_period() keeps: the snapshots from the period's opening state on and the flows after it, so the rebased period is the same as from the whole history. On a history of tens of thousands of days the views of a one-year report no longer pay for the days before it (the engine's daily states still do); the numbers are identical.

    🧵 Off the Event Loop

    DailyStateBuilder.build() is pure CPU, and a wide range makes it long: run on the event loop, one user's wide report held every other request until it ended (finding R13-SEC7, lot J-45). calculate() hands it to a worker thread instead, await loop.run_in_executor(_engine_executor(), contextvars.copy_context().run, builder.build):

    • A dedicated executor. _engine_executor(), in portfolio/engine.py, gives the module's ThreadPoolExecutor(max_workers=2, thread_name_prefix="portfolio-engine"), created on first use, not the default executor that every asyncio.to_thread() call of the backend shares. The app's shutdown step portfolio-engine (app_lifespan.SHUTDOWN_STEPS, after quant-pools) calls shutdown_engine_executor() (MF-J45a): a replay still queued is cancelled, nothing is waited for, and a later start in the same process makes a new executor. Its two workers bound how many replays run at once; a third waits in its queue. The GIL still runs one replay at a time, but the loop gets its turn in between: the interpreter switches threads every 5 ms (sys.getswitchinterval(), which the backend leaves at its default).
    • The copied context. Unlike asyncio.to_thread(), run_in_executor() does not copy the context variables: copy_context().run runs the replay in a copy of the request's context. Today that copy carries the secrets secrets_in_flight() masks in log lines, and the decimal context, which decimal keeps in a context variable: the replay computes with the precision and rounding of the request that started it. No code binds a structlog context today (merge_contextvars in logging_config.py merges nothing); one bound later reaches the replay too.
    • What crosses the thread. The builder reads loaded data only: the maps the earlier steps built, and the column attributes of the Transaction rows (id, date, type, broker_id, asset_id, quantity, amount, currency, cost_basis_currency, cost_basis_override). It reads no relationship: related_transaction loads lazily, and the builder never touches it. Transaction defers no column, and the builder reads only rows that calculate()'s own queries loaded, which never commits, rolls back or expires: no attribute reloads. No session enters the thread: every query of calculate() runs before the await, and no caller runs another task on the engine's session meanwhile. The replay's modules hold no mutable state, only constants and a logger. The portfolio_blob cache is written on the loop, after the await.
    • Cancellation. A thread cannot be cancelled. When the request is cancelled while its replay runs — its client disconnected — the replay runs to its end and its result is dropped, since the cache write comes after the await; a replay still waiting in the queue is cancelled before it starts. The range bounds of step 2 (R13-SEC7-1, R13-SEC8) bound the work.

    When you give the builder a new input, give it loaded data — a column already read, or a value an earlier step computed — never a lazy relationship or an expired attribute. The worker thread would try to load it outside the event loop, and the replay would fail with MissingGreenlet. test_engine_off_the_loop.py (test_financial/test_portfolio_engine/) pins the worker thread, the loop running meanwhile, the parity with a replay on the loop, the copied context, and a guard that fails on any lazy load during the replay.

    One build per key at a time (MF-J45b). Two identical requests that miss the cache together — the same user in two tabs, a refresh during a cold build, the PAC planner's allocation source beside the report — used to pay the reads and one of the two workers each. calculate() now keeps the builds in flight in _IN_FLIGHT, by blob key and exact range ((blob_key, actual_from, actual_to)). The cache also answers a request whose start is later than a stored blob's, but a flight does not: an identical request with a later start builds on its own.

    • the first request builds (_compute_uncached(): steps 6–12 above, unchanged), stores the result in the cache and hands it to the requests that arrived meanwhile, which await asyncio.shield() it: they get the same object, as a cache hit does, read-only;
    • if the build fails, every waiting request gets the same error at once (MF-J45d): the same data fails the same way, and retrying it one request after another made the k-th wait k builds before failing;
    • if the build's request is cancelled, the finally settles the entry with _LeaderGone before it removes it, so no request waits on a build nobody will finish: the first waiting request to resume builds for itself, the others wait for it;
    • a waiting request that is cancelled leaves the build alone (shield); other keys or ranges never wait for each other. The map is per process, like the cache.

    One run per page (FB-14). The Portfolio page asks the report (with the Performance table) and the positions chart for the same period; each used to call calculate() with its own flags, so the two requests had different blob keys and the page built the engine twice. PortfolioService._engine_run() is now the one call of get_report(), get_positions_contribution(), get_positions_series() and get_acquisition_funding_history(): always from t=0, with include_positions, include_acquisition_funding and period_start = the period's start. The requests of one page share the key: the first builds, the others read the cache or wait on the flight above. Each caller still serializes only what its query asks (a report without include_acquisition_funding has no funding series). The candles stay a flag of their own (include_candles), so their OHLC work stays off the ordinary reports; the extra outputs cost little (pure bookkeeping during the same replay). period_start stays in the key: a position frame does not carry everything a DailyPositionState does (reference prices, average cost, stale and missing-rate marks).

    The whole broker (MF6-3, developer decision D4). The ownership scaling (F2) is one function, native_cash.broker_shares(accesses, *, apply_ownership_share=True): by broker id, effective_share(role, share) — an OWNER counts its share_percentage (None = the whole broker), an EDITOR or VIEWER the whole broker — or 1 for every broker when apply_ownership_share is False, whatever the role and the share. calculate(apply_ownership_share=…) (default True) takes the flag, puts it in the blob-cache key, so the two views never answer for each other, and leaves the shares it counted in the result, PortfolioCalculationResult.broker_shares. get_summary() and get_positions_contribution() read their lot quantities at those shares (MF-A10), so their figures follow the run. _engine_run() passes the flag on: False is a run of its own, under its own key. The report and the positions series take it, POST /api/v1/portfolio/report (PortfolioReportQuery.apply_ownership_share, also in the report's L2 cache key) and POST /api/v1/portfolio/positions-series (PositionsSeriesQuery.apply_ownership_share), both defaulting to True: a caller that does not send it keeps the owner's share. False is for the broker page, which shows the broker's whole amounts, as an editor sees them; the Dashboard keeps the owner's share. A broker worth 10 000 € owned at 30 % is 3 000 € by default and 10 000 € with False; owned at 0 %, 0 € and 10 000 €. For one broker the ratios — TWRR, MWRR, simple ROI, weights, period percentages — are the same either way, since every flow and every value is multiplied by the same constant: only the amounts change (Portfolio Aggregation). With False every broker counts 1, so no pair links brokers owned at different shares and the run reports no TRANSFER_SHARE_MISMATCH (J-43). The lots analysis has no flag: it already counts whole amounts. The risk of a broker follows its page: PortfolioRiskScope.apply_ownership_share (default True) is passed by risk/scope.py to the report, so with False the scope value and the asset values the analytics read (the stress impacts among them) are the whole broker's, and an owner at 0 % still gets a risk; weights and returns are the same either way. Tests: test_broker_shares.py (test_financial_math/) and test_ownership_share_option.py (test_financial/).

    📅 The Days the Engine Recomputes

    DailyStateBuilder.build() runs one replay, with the same rules every day, from the earliest movement (or date_from, when it is earlier) to date_to: a movement dated before date_from moves the cash, the quantities, the cost pools, the capital and the K/R/W pools like any other — the pool rules of a cash movement that reduces no position are one static method, _apply_pool_rule(). A linked-internal CASH_TRANSFER or FX_CONVERSION leg goes through _move_linked_cash() (developer decision D-MF4): the departure records its (from R, amount) split in transfer_splits, keyed by its transaction id, and its arrival takes it up once, bringing back what the departure took from K and R. The gap between the two legs — a conversion's spread, the rate move while the cash travels, two brokers owned at different shares — is already in the engine's P&L, since no leg is an external flow: it is booked as a result (a gain to R, a loss out of R then K). An arrival whose departure is unknown arrives in K, and one whose departure is on the same day is applied last on that day. Any other CASH_TRANSFER or FX_CONVERSION leg — its partner outside the scope (linked_external_inflow / linked_external_outflow) or an orphan whose link was cleared or whose paired row is gone (normal) — is already an external flow of the capital baseline, and _cash_transfer_pools() moves its pools as that flow: an incoming leg through _deposit_pools() (it restores the withdrawn returns W into R first, the rest goes to K), an outgoing one through _withdrawal_pools() (K first, then R, which goes to W) — the same two helpers a DEPOSIT and a WITHDRAWAL use (B3, MF-J3). It never records or takes up a split in transfer_splits, and the capital baseline, the NAV and the P&L do not change; the cash breakdown (cash_from_contributed_capital / cash_from_generated_returns) and the BUY funding split of a broker-filtered view follow the pools it moved. The replay emits one DailyPortfolioState for every day of [date_from, date_to], so a later date_from gets for its days exactly the states of the full replay. It evaluates only the dirty days, which _collect_dirty_days() lists: any other day carries the previous state, dated that day, with no external flow and no acquisition funding. The rule is developer decision 28 of release 1.3, «recompute whenever a term of the formula changes». A replayed day is dirty when it has:

    • a cash movement, a transaction that moves a quantity, an external flow, or an internal flow between two brokers;
    • a change in the rate of a foreign currency held as cash, its first rate included (NativeCash.rate_change_days());
    • a change from the day before in a rate the day reads to value it (_valuation_rate_change_days()): the rate of every non-report currency of the assets and of their quotes, which values the securities, held and in transit, and the exchange part of the unrealized split; and, on the days of its transit, the rate of the departure currency of cash in transit. A rate that appears or disappears counts;
    • the departure day of a transit, its last day in transit, or its arrival day;
    • a market quote, even one equal to the previous close: the mark may have come from a trade the day before, and a new quote restarts the price's age;
    • the first day a carried price turns stale: each observation day — a quote, or a transaction that moves a quantity — plus STALE_PRICE_THRESHOLD_DAYS + 1, since a price is stale when it is carried more than STALE_PRICE_THRESHOLD_DAYS days;
    • a split;
    • or it is the first replayed day, date_from, or period_start when it falls in the replay.

    A quiet day is never visited: the loop jumps straight to the next dirty day (bisect.bisect_right() over the sorted dirty days; date_from is always one), so the replay costs its dirty days, not its calendar days, before and after date_from (P4).

    Segments (P4). PortfolioCalculationResult.daily_states is a DailyStates (models.py), a read-only Sequence of one state a day held as StateSegments: start (a computed day, with state.date == start), end (the last day it holds, included: the day before the next start, or date_to) and state. Inside a segment every field keeps its value except date, external_cash_flow (0) and acquisition_funding (None), because every day on which another field can change is dirty. Indexing, slicing and iteration build a quiet day when it is read (StateSegment.state_on(): dataclasses.replace(), its containers copied), so the output is the one a day-by-day walk gives, while memory grows with the computed days only. Measured on a copy of the test database with one deposit typed in 1500: 192 413 states and 686 MB kept before P4, 397 segments and 5 MB after. A reader that needs the values, not one object a day, asks one day (state_on(), state_as_of()) or walks iter_segments(date_from, date_to) (segments clipped to the window): period.state_as_of(), the data-quality report's incomplete-NAV days, _engine_missing_fx_pairs(), and the derived views, whose windows are found by date arithmetic and read day by day from their segment's state (DerivedViewsBuilder._window_days(), the day's date taken from the walk, never from the state). Nobody mutates a state or its containers after the build.

    Position frames (#48). With include_positions (calculate(), part of the blob-cache key) or compute_position_frames (the builder), the result carries position_frames: for each held position, PositionFrames (broker_id, asset_id, start, end included, quantity at the owner's share, market_value, cost_basis, cost_complete, valuation_source, unrealized_pnl) — the values _build_position_state() gives each day, merged while none of them changes (Decimals compared with their exponent). A frame changes only with its own position: a quote of another asset does not split it. A position not held on a day has no frame there, so a closed position simply ends. The frames are grouped in the order the replay first met each position, each group by start: summing from Decimal("0"), in that order, the market values (not None) of the frames covering a day gives that day's market_value exactly. Off by default.

    Held positions (MF-A6). A position is held when its quantity, at the owner's share, is above QUANTITY_DUST_THRESHOLD (platform/data_quality_thresholds.py, 0.00001 units): _is_held() in daily_state.py, the predicate of the Performance rows (PortfolioService.get_positions_contribution()) and of the position series. The builder asks it in three places — the day's valuation loop, the snapshot at the end of period_start (position_states_start) and the one at date_to (position_states_end) — so a residual at or below the threshold has no market value, no cost in open_cost_basis, no by_asset or by_type amount, no part of its broker's nav_contribution, no frame and no snapshot entry, and, unpriced, it does not put the day in missing_price_asset_ids nor make nav_complete False. Its cost leaves the open cost basis without a sale, so the period identity puts it in the residual Other (§7): a sale of 9.99999 of 10 units bought at 100 000 leaves 0.00001, worth 1.20 at 120 000 and costing 1.00 — the NAV leaves it out, Σ rows' ΔUGL equals the portfolio's, and Other is −1.00. Before, the state valued every quantity above zero: the NAV counted the 1.20 and ΔUGL a 0.20 that no row had. The quantity itself stays in cumulative_qty: dust is a rule of valuation, not of the ledger. Tests: test_daily_state_dust_threshold.py (the engine) and test_positions_contribution_dust_identity.py (the Performance table against the summary).

    The positions chart (#48). position_series.build_position_series() turns the frames of the chosen positions into one series each, as steps (a point holds from its date to its through), and nothing else of the engine: it reads the realized sales, the internal transfers, the cost of what was bought (acquisitions_from_average_costs()) and the income and costs by day (NativeCash.position_flow_steps()). Its pnl on a day is the Performance row's period_pnl of the period from date_from to that day, by the same function (contributions.position_period_pnl), so the last point of a series is the table's row. Its base, the capital the percent is on, is the value at date_from in absolute terms, plus the cost of what is bought after it, plus what an internal transfer brings in: at its value on arrival when the period splits its tract (contributions.counted_tract, the units' pnl is then measured from that value), else at the cost moved, as the table's base does; a sale never lowers it, and a base that is 0 or unknown gives no percent (the developer's decision of 23/10). The sales it reads are the table's, period.period_realized_sales, and the tract of a transfer is contributions.transfer_tract: the series keeps no copy of either rule. The steps cover every calendar day of a position from its first to its last day, without a gap (D-P4): the days a position's figures can change on are those of its inputs (a frame starting or ending, a sale, an income or a cost, a purchase, a transfer's tract or arrival, the period's start), each read as ever by a lookup, and two consecutive points with the same figures, down to the exponent of each Decimal, are one (series_steps.steps_from_rows). Expanding every point over its days gives the daily series field for field; a date typed in the year 1 costs what the changes cost, not 740 000 points. A position below the dust threshold is not held, and its point is 0, as in the engine's day totals (held positions, above). A closed position ends the day it went to 0, with its final pnl; units sent to another broker of the scope stay the sender's until they arrive (P22), so its series goes on to the arrival, holding the pnl of the last day held, and the tract settles it there. An asset asked without a broker is the sum over the brokers that held it: the tracts of a transfer between them cancel and what it brings in is not new capital. A value that is not known leaves market_value None and pnl the sum of the parts that are known, with complete False, as the row does. PortfolioService.get_positions_series() reads the page's run (_engine_run(), one run per page: the report of the same period built it, and the blob cache keeps it between two selections) and serves POST /api/v1/portfolio/positions-series.

    The lots' histories as steps (D-P4). The seven daily series of POST /portfolio/lots/analysis (value, return, price, market price, broker and cumulative average cost, performance) give a row for each stretch of days in which nothing changes: it holds from its date to its through, both included, and the response has the range they lie in. The analysis never walks the calendar. The days the converted market price can change on are known from the inputs (AssetPriceSeries.observation_dates, the steps of the rates, FxRateResolver.rate_change_days(), the events and the end of the days valued at cost, each with the day after it: lots/change_days.price_candidate_days); the price is read on those, the builders run on the days lot_change_days keeps, and history_steps turns their rows into steps per key: consecutive identical rows, with the exponent of every Decimal, are one, and a change day where a key has no row after its first opens a gap. The days between two reads repeat the day before to the digit, so expanding the steps gives the daily answer field for field. A missing rate is a gap of the pair over the stretch it holds.

    The asset dimension. Each state carries by_asset (asset id → the held market value summed into market_value, every broker; no cash). DerivedViewsBuilder.build_asset_allocation_current() and build_asset_allocation_history() turn it into allocation slices named by the caller (id = the asset id), plus the Liquidity slice of by_type (cash and cash in transit): the report's summary.allocation_by_asset and allocation_history.asset.

    Allocation slices (MF-C2). Every allocation dimension — type, sector, geography, asset; current and history — takes its percentages from weights.slice_percents(): each slice's amount over the signed sum of the day's slices, × 100, unrounded, in the mapping's order; DerivedViewsBuilder rounds them to 0.01 (_cents()). A slice keeps its sign, so with negative cash and a positive sum the cash slice is below zero and the others add up to more than 100 ({Stock: 1500, Liquidity: −500} → 150 / −50). When the sum is zero or negative, no slice has a share: slice_percents() returns None for every slice and _cents() keeps it, while name, amount, id, emoji and the order (by amount, descending) are unchanged — stocks bought on an overdraft ({Stock: 500, Liquidity: −500}), or an emptied portfolio ({Liquidity: 0}). The contracts carry the None: AllocationItem.value (schemas/portfolio.py, also inside AllocationHistoryPoint.components) and the AI Export AllocationSlice.percent (ai_export/components/payloads/portfolio_broker.py) are nullable, serialised as JSON null; the broker concentration context sorts a slice without a percent as 0 (_map_slices()). Before, the sum was replaced by 1 when it was zero (500 read 50 000 %) and a negative sum flipped every sign. An empty allocation has no slices. Tests: test_weights.py, test_allocation_non_positive_sum.py (the views) and test_allocation_non_positive_sum_report.py (through PortfolioService).

    The engine has no date floor (lot MF-PAV-L1): a movement stored in 1500, or in year 1, is replayed exactly, on its own date and in its own order, and the states start at the first transaction. When the requested date_from is later than a movement, the replay starts at the movement and crosses the years between it and date_from from dirty day to dirty day. The old counter rows_before_floor (RowsBeforeFloor) and the data-quality issue TRANSACTIONS_BEFORE_FLOOR no longer exist: nothing is moved or dropped, so there is nothing to warn about.

    Why: R13-SEC8

    A transaction stored with a date far in the past — year 1, say — made every replay walk every calendar day since: a state carried on each quiet day, a rate calendar for each currency, a rate request for each day of each foreign currency it values (finding R13-SEC8, lot MF-SEC8-2). A row dated 0001-01-01 could even raise OverflowError: a rate calendar starts the day before the replay, and date.min has none. The floor of 1 January 1900 that lot MF-SEC8-2 put in front of the replay is gone: the cost no longer depends on the calendar days (rates are read as steps, calendars hold only the candidate days), and a calendar has no day before date.min.

    Both rate rules take their days from one pure function, the one home of «the day a rate changes»: change_days.change_days(read, currencies, days) (moved from native_cash) returns the days of the calendar days, in its order, on which read(currency, day) gives one of currencies another rate than on the calendar's previous day; a rate on the calendar's first day counts. The caller chooses the reading and the calendar. The native cash reads the last rate known on or before the day, on the calendar of its rate points. The engine reads the rate fx_rate_map holds for that exact day (_read_rate()), on every replayed day — or day of the transit, for cash in transit — each compared with the day before; its calendars start at the replay's start, and each holds only the candidate days (_reading_calendar(): the steps and window bounds of the rates, each with the day before it, from change_days.reading_change_days()), never every day since. _read_rate() reads only the map, which _preload_fx_rates() already answers for these currencies on every day of [date_from, date_to] (a day replayed before a later date_from may find none there, and its state is not emitted), and for cash in transit on every day of its transit, from the interval's own start: the rule asks the FX service for nothing more.

    change_days.py is the one home of the day rules every daily series shares: reading_change_days(rates, source, destination) gives the days a reading of a pair may differ from the day before (an AskedDayRates' steps and window bounds, or, on a plain map built by hand in tests, each key's day and the day after), change_days() the days a reading really differs on a calendar, with_next_days(days, calendar) the days inside a calendar with the day after each. The engine's _reading_calendar() and the lots' FxRateResolver.rate_change_days() take their candidate days from reading_change_days(); lots/change_days.py (lot_change_days(), price_candidate_days()) adds the day after with with_next_days(). The unions stay with their domain: the engine's dirty days are _collect_dirty_days(), which compares rates, and the lots' change days compare the converted price in its exact representation, so that their series stay byte-identical.

    When you add an input that can change a day's value — a rate, a price, a mark's source, an age — add its days to the dirty days, or show that the day cannot change. test_fx_only_days.py (test_services/test_financial/test_portfolio_engine/, run by ./dev.py test services roi-fifo-utils) moves one term at a time on an otherwise quiet day, checks with its guards that a day whose terms do not move keeps its values, and tests change_days() under both readings.

    Before release 1.3

    Until lot CP-6 (backlog B11), a day on which only a foreign security's rate moved (or the rate of cash in transit in a currency the native cash did not hold), a quote equal to the previous close replaced a trade mark or restarted the price's age, or a carried price turned stale, was a copy of the last dirty day.     🔻 The First Negative Cash Day

    native_cash.first_negative_cash(movements, *, opening=None, check_days=()) answers one question for one broker: on which day, first, does its balance in a currency end below zero? It returns a frozen NegativeCash — day, currency, balance (the currency's balance at the end of that day) and reducers (that day's movements of that currency that lowered it, in input order, so the caller picks the one to blame) — or None when no balance goes below zero. It is the cash half of the balance validator (transactions/balances.py, _validate_broker_balances(), lot W6-12), which it is written for; the validator does not call it yet.

    Input Meaning
    movements CashMovements of one broker, in any order; movements of more than one broker raise ValueError. The balance is the counted amount, CashMovement.native
    opening The balance of each currency before the first movement — native_balances() of the earlier rows, the validator's _get_balances_before_date()
    check_days Days to check even without a movement, such as the validator's first day
    • End of day. Each day's movements are applied in their order, then every balance is checked, so a deposit and a payment on the same day never read as red in between.
    • Only the days that matter. The walk visits the days with a movement and the check_days, never the days in between: a movement dated 9999-12-31 costs one step (R13-SEC7-3).
    • Which currency. On a day with several currencies below zero, the first one met wins: opening's order, then the order the walk first met each currency.
    • Only cash. Quantities are the caller's.

    Tests: test_first_negative_cash.py (test_services/test_financial_math/), run by ./dev.py test services financial-math.

    🧾 Ledger Control Totals

    ledger_totals.py reduces the transaction ledger to its control totals — the cash of every broker in each currency and the quantity of every position — read from the rows alone, with no price, rate, cost, setting or clock. The instance backup writes them into a backup's manifest. The check of a restore recomputes them on the archive's copy, once upgraded to the app's schema, and compares them before the restore is confirmed. A difference is a warning in the check's report, never a refusal: a migration may correct data.

    Name Contract
    CONTROL_TOTALS_VERSION 1. It names the method — what is summed and how it is written — so a change to either bumps it: totals of another version are not comparable
    ControlTotals(version, cash, quantities) Frozen dataclass with slots, compared by value. cash holds (broker_id, currency, balance) lines and quantities (broker_id, asset_id, quantity) lines, each sorted by its key, with no zero line: an absent line is zero
    position_quantities(transactions, as_of=None) Pure. {broker_id: {asset_id: quantity}} over the rows dated on or before as_of (every row when None), by the portfolio engine's rule: every row with an asset and a non-zero quantity, whatever its type. Like native_balances(), it keeps a line whose rows cancel out, at zero
    control_totals(transactions) Pure. The cash is native_balances() over cash_movement_from_transaction() of every row (a row without a currency moves no cash), the quantities are position_quantities(). The sums are exact: they run in a local decimal context that raises decimal.Inexact instead of rounding, and leave the caller's context as it was
    compute_control_totals(session) Async. The totals of the whole ledger session reads: a named read point of the layer (rule 1)

    compute_control_totals() reads through the session it is given and nothing else: only the columns the totals need (id, type, date, broker_id, asset_id, currency, amount, quantity), in keyset blocks of _BLOCK_SIZE (5,000) rows by primary key. It sums in Python, never with an SQL SUM: on SQLite a Numeric column goes through a float. It reads no price, rate or setting, makes no network call, never asks for today's date and keeps no module cache, so it can run on a session opened on another SQLite file — a backup's copy of the database — in the same process as the live one.

    to_json() writes the version, then the lines in order, with the keys below; for the reference ledger of the tests:

    {
  "version": 1,
  "cash": [
    {"broker_id": 1, "currency": "EUR", "balance": "278.5"},
    {"broker_id": 1, "currency": "USD", "balance": "545.5"},
    {"broker_id": 2, "currency": "EUR", "balance": "107"},
    {"broker_id": 2, "currency": "USD", "balance": "510"}
  ],
  "quantities": [
    {"broker_id": 1, "asset_id": 10, "quantity": "1"},
    {"broker_id": 2, "asset_id": 10, "quantity": "4"},
    {"broker_id": 2, "asset_id": 11, "quantity": "0.000001"}
  ]
}

    Every amount is canonical decimal text — plain notation without trailing zeros ("510", not "510.000000"; "100", not "1E+2"), "0" for zero, never "-0" — so one ledger has one text. ControlTotals.from_json(data) is the inverse of to_json() and raises ValueError for another version or a malformed document. Read version before calling it: totals of another version are not comparable, which is not the same as a difference.

    from_json() reads an amount only as to_json() writes it.

    • Accepted: an optional -, then at most 60 ASCII digits before the point and 60 after it, the precision of the exact context.
    • Refused with ValueError: everything else that Decimal would also take — an exponent ("1E+9"), a +, spaces, _, other scripts' digits.
    • Why: a manifest written by hand, or damaged, could otherwise make _canonical() write an exponent out in full; "1E+50000000" alone costs about 121 MB.
    • Round trip: it holds for every total within that bound, and every ledger the database can hold stays within it (amounts RealDecimal(18, 6), quantities RealDecimal(22, 10)).

    What the totals promise their caller:

    • The whole instance. Every broker at 100% and every row, whatever its date: no user, share or scope.
    • Blind to the row type. A migration that reclassifies the halves of a TRANSFER as ADJUSTMENTs, or those of a CASH_TRANSFER as a WITHDRAWAL and a DEPOSIT, changes nothing; nor do a row's date, its transaction id or the order the rows are read in.
    • A difference means a row lost, added or altered in broker, asset, currency, amount or quantity.
    • Dust is kept. A quantity at or below QUANTITY_DUST_THRESHOLD (services/platform/data_quality_thresholds.py), which the Dashboard does not list as a holding, keeps its line: a control total sees every unit.
    • No cost basis. cost_basis_override is never read: a cost basis depends on rates and on the WAC and FIFO rules, and carrying it would bump the version at every change of those rules.
    • Ids local to the instance. Lines are keyed by broker_id and asset_id, which mean something only inside one instance: the totals compare a restore of the whole instance with its own backup.

    test_ledger_totals.py, in test_services/test_financial_math/ (services financial-math), pins this contract on a reference ledger — each promise above, the canonical text, the exact sums, the parity with the average-cost fold and native_balances() — and runs compute_control_totals() on private SQLite files under tmp_path, several in one process, as a restore check reads them. Its static guard parses the module's source: no SQL SUM, no settings, module cache, FX service, price, rate or clock.

    1. A calculation owns its whole problem. It takes the plain data of the problem — movements, currencies, dates — and calls the infrastructure it needs to complete it: fx.conversion.convert_bulk() for conversions, the base currency from settings_service, the thresholds of data_quality_thresholds, the cache. Its caller does not prepare values for it: compute_average_costs() receives the amounts actually paid and converts each acquisition itself, at its own date, in one batched call. A calculation reads its own data from the database only at named points: the portfolio engine (portfolio/engine.py, with load_configured_fx_pair_sets() in portfolio/data_quality.py for the configured FX routes), the Yield on Cost loaders (yield_on_cost.py), the ledger control totals (compute_control_totals(), through the session it is given) and the split links (splits.load_split_links() and splits.split_linked_ids(), the rows of the transactions it is given; splits.costless_incoming_split_adjustments(), the rows linked to the events it is given).
    2. Callers call the layer, never the other way round. The layer never imports the orchestration services (portfolio_service, broker_service, transaction_service, lots_analysis_service), the API, AI Export, risk, PAC or signals: they call it.
    3. No silent fallback. A missing rate, price or cost is reported — MissingConversion (average cost), MissingCashConversion (native cash), PortfolioCalculationResult.missing_fx (portfolio engine), a data-quality issue — and the value is unavailable: never a number, never the unconverted amount.

    4. No re-exports. Every __init__.py of the layer holds only its docstring. Import a name from the module that defines it:

      # ✅ From the module that defines the name

# ❌ Not from the package: its __init__.py exports nothing

      Tests replace module globals — convert_bulk above all — on the module that reads them. from … import … binds the name in the importing module when it loads: replacing it anywhere else, in the FX service or in a package that re-exported it, leaves that binding untouched, and the patch is mute. 5. One implementation. Before writing a calculation, search the layer. If it exists, call it, with parameters when your case differs: cumulative_to_annualized(total_return, days, min_days=…) takes its 30-day floor as a parameter. Never keep a local or partial copy elsewhere, and report any copy you find. 6. The frontend computes none of these quantities. It shows the fields the backend computes. Interactive measures are the only exceptions — the chart measuring tool (MeasureSignal.ts) and the what-if curves of the synthetic benchmarks (CompoundSignal.ts, LinearSignal.ts, SineSignal.ts), all in frontend/src/lib/charts/signals/; and the lot measures shaped by the user's choices (D-MF6(b)): the lots table footer over the selection, else the filtered rows (unifiedLotsTableHelpers.ts), the comparison chart's aggregate of the selected lots (lotComparisonChartHelpers.ts) and the WAC chart's percentage view (lotWacPriceChartHelpers.ts), all in frontend/src/lib/features/brokers/lots/; and the return chart of the positions the user ticks (Y5-2, frontend/src/lib/features/dashboard/positionsSeries.ts), which shows the backend's fraction in «base 100» and moves the Dashboard's history to the left edge of the user's window — a unit and a shift of numbers the backend computed, not a new calculation; and the trade amount of the transaction form (frontend/src/lib/features/transactions/form/txTradeAmount.ts, W5-9): the amount from the unit price the user types, or the unit price from the amount, quantity / quote_base_quantity × price on exact decimals (BigInt at a fixed scale, half-even to the stored 6 and 12 decimals, no float and no library) — a single product or quotient, which the developer decided (25/10/2026) is the frontend's own and does not deserve a route. The line is the size of the calculation: a small sum or product on the numbers the user types stays in the browser; anything with a series, a date, a rate or a rule is the backend's. Each exception must be declared where it lives. The measuring tool takes the backend's annualization floor: MIN_ANNUALIZATION_DAYS in MeasureSignal.ts is held to _MIN_ANNUALIZATION_DAYS of returns.py by a parity test in measureSignal.test.ts, so changing one without the other fails that test. Its changePct (null on a zero start, N65) is also the one Δ% rule of the chart tooltip (priceChartHelpers.ts::buildDeltaHtml, J-44), which imports it rather than keeping a copy. The declaration is the tag @financial-math-exception in the file's leading JSDoc block, followed by what the exception computes and why it stays in the browser. The gate (frontend/src/financialMathExceptions.gate.test.ts) holds the list of declared files and fails when a listed file lacks the tag, an unlisted file carries it, or an annualizing exponent with 365 (Math.pow(…, …365…), ** …365… or **= …365…) appears outside the listed files that annualize: adding an exception means extending that list on purpose; otherwise, use the backend's field.

    1. Search. Look in the package map and grep the layer, for example grep -rn "annualiz" backend/app/services/financial_math/. If the calculation exists, call it.
